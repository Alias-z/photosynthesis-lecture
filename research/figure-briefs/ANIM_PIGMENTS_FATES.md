## Animation 3 · "Why pigments differ" (played from slide 4b, leads into slide 5 "Why are leaves green?")

Simplification, stated honestly: the absorbing electrons are drawn as spread over a "room", the conjugated system (the chain or ring of alternating single and double bonds). A bigger room means more closely spaced energy levels, so a smaller gap, so redder light is absorbed. This is the textbook free-electron model (Kuhn 1949). Real orbital shapes are not drawn.

| # | Beat | What you see | Caption (draft) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Into the molecule | Zoom from leaf → chloroplast → one chlorophyll; its ring of alternating bonds glows | Chlorophyll's absorbing electrons spread over its ring of alternating single and double bonds. |
| 2 | The room sets the gap | Three chains with 3, 5 and 11 double bonds; each grows a ladder whose gap shrinks; the matching photon moves from ultraviolet to blue | A longer chain gives the electrons more room: smaller gaps, so longer wavelengths are absorbed. |
| 3 | Carotenoids | β-carotene's long chain; a blue-green photon fits its gap and is absorbed | Carotenoids' long chain absorbs blue to blue-green light. |
| 4 | Chl a → Chl b | One –CH₃ on the ring swaps to –CHO; the ladder's rungs nudge: red band 662 → 642 nm, blue band 430 → 453 nm | Chlorophyll b differs by one group, which shifts both of its absorption bands. |
| 5 | Together | The three absorption spectra stack and cover more of the visible band; green stays weakest | Different structures, different gaps: together they catch more light. |

## Animation 4 · "Fates of an excitation" (played from slide 12)

| # | Beat | What you see | Caption (draft) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Absorb | Zoom into a chlorophyll's energy ladder: blue lifts the electron to S₂, red to S₁ | A photon lifts an electron to an excited state in femtoseconds. |
| 2 | Relax | S₂ → S₁ as a heat wiggle; a clock shows < 1 ps | Extra energy is lost as heat within a picosecond; every excitation ends in S₁. |
| 3 | Photochemistry | The energy hops to an open reaction centre; an electron leaves (charge separation, < 100 ps) | With the reaction centre open, most excitations end in photochemistry. |
| 4 | Fluorescence | Reaction centre closed: a slightly redder photon (~685 nm) leaves after nanoseconds | With the reaction centre closed, more energy leaves as fluorescence. |
| 5 | Heat (NPQ) | In strong light, a quencher turns the excitation into heat | In excess light, protective quenching (NPQ) turns more of it into heat. |
| 6 | Triplet | The electron's spin flips → T₁ (lives µs); it passes energy to O₂ → singlet oxygen; a carotenoid quenches it | Rarely, a triplet forms and can make damaging singlet oxygen; carotenoids quench it. |
