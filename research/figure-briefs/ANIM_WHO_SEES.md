# Animation 2, "Who uses which light?": draft script v3 (awaiting the lecturer's approval; do not build)

It plays after the slide 3 sky page and leads to slide 4 (the solar spectrum). See `ORDER_PROPOSAL.md` v2 for the order.

## Brief

- **Length:** about 33 s.
- **Examples:** us (from the sea to primates), then bees, then plants, the lecture's subject.
- **Opening:** none needed. Animation 1 has already shown that sunlight is a mix of wavelengths, so v2's "Sunlight" beat is cut.
- **Ending:** the question that slides 4 and 5 answer: which band, and why that one?
- **Style:** the slide style: Bristol paper, our fonts, and our sun, leaf and eye drawings.
- **The thread:** a spectrum ribbon (ultraviolet · visible · infrared) runs across the top of the whole animation. Each beat highlights the slice it is about.

## Claims deliberately left out

- **"Eyes match the Sun's peak."** The peak's position is an artefact of the axis units.
- **"Water shaped our visual range."** This is plausible but unproven; the script only states what water absorbs.
- **"Primates gained red–green vision when life moved onto land."** Vertebrates reached land hundreds of millions of years before primates. Red–green vision came much later and only in primates.
- **"Red algae live deeper because of their colour."** Contested.
- **Snakes and other animals.** Cut to keep the examples few.
