## 2. Accuracy check of the 4b/5 wording proposed earlier today

| Earlier wording | Precise version | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Energy above S₁ is lost as heat" | Only the **excess** above S₁ becomes heat, in under 1 ps. The S₁ energy itself then drives photochemistry or leaves as fluorescence, heat or a triplet (slide 12) | Internal conversion is faster than emission, so fluorescence comes only from S₁ (Kasha's rule) | Bricker 2015; Kasha 1950 |
| "Most of blue's extra energy, some of green's" | All the excess goes to heat: blue (430 nm) ≈ 1.0 eV, green (550 nm) ≈ 0.4 eV, red (662 nm) ≈ 0 | E = hc/λ, with S₁ = 1.87 eV | calc |
| "Green falls between them" | Chlorophyll has only weak transitions in the green, so it absorbs green weakly | The bands are broad (vibrational sublevels); green is not exactly "between levels" | Reimers 2013 |
| "Every absorbed photon counts once" | Each photon absorbed **by chlorophyll** leaves one S₁ excitation. Carotenoids pass only part of theirs to chlorophyll; other pigments (e.g. anthocyanins) none | Not every absorbed photon reaches chlorophyll | Polívka & Frank 2010; Hogewoning 2012 |
| "S₁ ≈ 1.9 eV" | ≈ 1.8 eV in the leaf (≈ 680 nm); 1.87 eV in solvent (662 nm) | Chlorophyll absorbs further to the red when bound to protein | calc |
| "Proteins hold every pigment ~1 nm apart" | Proteins hold the chlorophylls and carotenoids; neighbouring chlorophylls are ≈ 1 nm apart | The 1 nm is the average Chl–Chl distance; carotenoids sit closer | van Amerongen & Croce 2013 |
| "The longer this chain, the redder the light" | The longer the system of alternating bonds, the smaller the gap, so the pigment absorbs longer wavelengths (lower-energy photons); clearest in carotenoids | "Redder" was ambiguous: it could be read as the colour the pigment looks. Chlorophyll's colour comes from its ring system, not a chain | Britton 1995; Kuhn 1949 |

## 3. Proposed slide content (assuming option A)

### Slide 4b · Excitation

| Part | Proposed wording |
|---|---|
| Key message | Whatever its colour, a photon absorbed by chlorophyll leaves one excitation in S₁; only the excess above S₁ becomes heat |
| Lead | A pigment absorbs a photon only if the photon's energy matches an electron transition (a gap between energy levels) |
| Point 1 | **Absorption needs a match.** Red photons lift an electron to S₁, blue to a higher state (Sₙ) / Chlorophyll has only weak transitions in the green, so it absorbs green weakly |
| Point 2 | **Only the excess becomes heat.** Energy above S₁ is lost as heat in under 1 ps / Blue loses ≈ 1.0 eV, green ≈ 0.4 eV, red almost none |
| Point 3 | **Everything starts from S₁.** Each photon absorbed by chlorophyll leaves one S₁ excitation (≈ 1.8 eV) / From S₁ it drives photochemistry, or leaves as fluorescence, heat or a triplet (slide 12) |
| History | Kasha (1950): light is emitted only from the lowest excited state. That is why chlorophyll fluoresces red (~685 nm), whatever colour it absorbed |
| Sources | Reimers 2013; Bricker 2015; Kasha 1950; van Amerongen & Croce 2013 |

### Slide 5 · Pigments

| Part | Proposed wording |
|---|---|
| Key message | A pigment's structure sets the colours it absorbs; chlorophylls and carotenoids absorb all colours but green poorly, and proteins in the thylakoid membrane hold them |
| Title | Pigments: structure sets colour |
| Lead | A pigment's colour comes from its structure: which electron transitions it has |
| Point 1 | **Longer conjugation, longer wavelengths absorbed.** The absorbing electrons spread over alternating single and double bonds / The longer this system, the smaller the gap, so lower-energy (longer-wavelength) photons are absorbed |
| Point 2 | **Chlorophylls and carotenoids.** Chl a and b absorb blue and red; Chl b's –CHO shifts both bands / Carotenoids absorb blue to blue-green and pass energy to chlorophyll; together they absorb least in green |
| Point 3 | **Bound to proteins.** Proteins in the thylakoid membrane hold the chlorophylls and carotenoids / Neighbouring chlorophylls are ≈ 1 nm apart, so energy can hop between them |
| History | Tswett (1906) invents chromatography to separate a leaf's pigments by colour → cryo-EM places every pigment: 105 chlorophylls and 28 carotenoids in one PSII–LHCII half (Wei 2016) |
| Sources | Britton 1995; Kuhn 1949; Lichtenthaler 1987; Polívka & Frank 2010; van Amerongen & Croce 2013; Wei 2016 |
