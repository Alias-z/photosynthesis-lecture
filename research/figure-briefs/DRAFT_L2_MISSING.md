## 1. Rubisco must be activated (C2)

**Point 1, new** (replaces "A short-lived six-carbon step"; figure a already shows "6-C intermediate stays bound"):
- **Rubisco must be switched on.** A CO₂ and Mg²⁺ bind its active site first; activase uses ATP to clear blocked sites. In heat, Rubisco deactivates.
- Option 1b: keep the six-carbon point and put activation in the lead ("Once activated by CO₂ and Mg²⁺, Rubisco adds CO₂ to…"). This is shorter, but the lead becomes two ideas.
- **Sources:** Lorimer et al. 1976 (Abs: CO₂ + Mg²⁺ form a carbamate); Yamori 2026 (Abs: activase is ATP-dependent and releases inhibitors); Scafaro et al. 2023 (Abs: above the optimum temperature, Rubisco deactivation and lower J co-limit uptake).

## 5. Rubisco engineering status (C5)

**Point 3, new second line:**
- **A hard reaction, not a bad enzyme.** Typical for its chemistry; at ≈ 0.7 Gt Earth's most abundant enzyme. More Rubisco raised yields by ≈ 10 % in first field trials; faster lab variants are not yet in crops.

**Sources:**
- Salesse-Smith et al. 2025 (Abs: increasing Rubisco content gave c. 10 % in initial field trials, in rice, maize, sorghum and sugarcane).
- McDonald et al. 2026 (FT by reviewer B: lab variants with higher efficiency, untested in plants). No number is quoted on the slide.

## 8. CO₂ compensation point Γ (C7 defines it, C10 compares it)

**C7, point 2, new:**
- **A chamber sees only the net.** A = V_c − 0.5 V_o − R_L: CO₂ fixed minus CO₂ released. A = 0 at the CO₂ compensation point Γ (≈ 50 µmol mol⁻¹ in C₃ leaves at 25 °C).

**C10, figure h** ("C₃ → C₂ → C₄", three leaves on one anatomy):
- Under each leaf, replace the schematic CO₂ meter caption with measured Γ: "Γ ≥ 40 · 12–26 · ≤ 10 µl l⁻¹".
- Key: "survey of several hundred species (Krenzer et al. 1975)".
- C₂'s 12–26 comes from the two "misfits" in that survey (Moricandia 26, Panicum milioides 12–20).

**Sources:** Busch et al. 2024 (FT by reviewer D: typical Γ ≈ 50, Γ\* ≈ 40 µmol mol⁻¹ at 25 °C); Krenzer et al. 1975 (Abs, re-read today).

## 9. Why C₄ wins in heat and low CO₂ (C10)

**Point 3, new:**
- **A trade-off that pays in heat and low CO₂.** About 8,100 species (maize, sugarcane) do one-fifth of land photosynthesis; the first grass C₄ arose as CO₂ fell, 32–25 million years ago.

**Figures:** figure e ("Cost crossover") already shows the mechanism ("oxygenations per carboxylation in a C₃ leaf: rises with warming and with less CO₂ at Rubisco"). Add one marker on its x-axis: "C₃ and C₄ quantum yields equal near 30 °C".

**"More than 60 times"** moves to figure j (C₄ rice parts), where it is already written.

**Sources:**

- Ehleringer & Björkman 1977 (Abs in the 26 Oct review: C₃ 0.052 vs C₄ 0.053 mol CO₂ per absorbed photon at 30 °C).

## 10. Water cost of CO₂ uptake (C7)

**Point 1, new second line:**
- **Stomata gate CO₂ and cost water.** A = g_s (C_a − C_i), with g_s the conductance to CO₂ (water vapour's ÷ 1.6). Each CO₂ taken up costs ≈ 200 water molecules (25 °C, 50 % humidity).

**Source:** Calc: E / A = 1.6 × VPD / (C_a − C_i).
- VPD at 25 °C and 50 % RH is 1.58 kPa, or 0.0156 mol mol⁻¹.
- C_a − C_i is 420 − 300 = 120 µmol mol⁻¹ (typical values, Busch et al. 2024).
- That gives E / A ≈ 208.
- Leaf and air are assumed to be at the same temperature. The slide would mark the number "calculated".

- This also applies the pending wording fix: "C₄ chemistry" was imprecise.
