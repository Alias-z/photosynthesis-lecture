## 2. Slide 7 · route overview with the compartment map

**Wording**
- **Title:** "Two light-driven steps, one electron route" (unchanged).
- **Lead:** "Two photosystems in series carry electrons from water to NADP⁺, each giving them a light-driven boost." (unchanged)
- **Points:**
  - **Mobile carriers link the complexes.** PQ in the membrane, PC in the lumen, Fd in the stroma / FNR passes Fd's electrons to NADP⁺

## 3. Slide 11 · three outlets, with the FLV story

**Wording**
- **Title:** "Electron routes make the system flexible" (unchanged).
- **Lead:** "After PSI, electrons go to NADPH (linear), back to PQ (cyclic) or to O₂; the mix sets how many H⁺ are pumped per NADPH and keeps PSI safe."
- **Points:**
  - **Linear flow alone is short of ATP.** ≈ 1.3 ATP per NADPH (14 H⁺ per 3 ATP) / the Calvin–Benson cycle needs 1.5
  - **Cyclic flow adds H⁺, not NADPH.** PGR5 (main) and NDH return electrons to PQ / PGR5 protects PSI; whether C₃ leaves need cyclic flow for ATP is debated
  - **Flowering plants lost the fast O₂ outlet.** Cyanobacteria to conifers pass surplus electrons to O₂ with flavodiiron proteins (FLV) / In flowering plants the water–water cycle takes < 10 % of electrons
- **Sources:** Kramer & Evans 2011 · Badger et al. 2000 · Ilík et al. 2017 · Suorsa et al. 2012 · Degen et al. 2026.
- **In the figure's own key:** Asada 1999 · Yamamoto et al. 2016 · Wada et al. 2018 · Rizzetto et al. 2025.

**What changes**
- "PGR5 protects PSI" joins the cyclic point, which frees point 3 for the outlet that flowering plants lost.
- The water–water cycle is named.
- The malate valve appears in the figure only, as one arrow: "spare NADPH leaves as malate".

**Figure: five variants.** One ink per route as now: linear red, cyclic violet, to O₂ teal. The to-O₂ route in flowering plants is always drawn thin.

**Talk track (spoken, not on the slide):**
- 1951: Mehler sees lit chloroplasts consume O₂.
- 1999: Asada names the water–water cycle.
- 2003: in cyanobacteria the O₂ outlet turns out to be a different enzyme, FLV, which makes no reactive oxygen.
- 2017: every plant group keeps FLV except flowering plants.
- 2016–2025: FLV is put back into Arabidopsis, rice and tobacco, where it protects PSI in fluctuating light.
- Why it was lost is open: other routes may have taken over, and in moss too much FLV slows growth (Alboresi et al. 2018; Traverso et al. 2025).

## Checked sources (26 Oct 2026)
- **Wietrzynski et al. 2025, eLife** (full text): stromal gap 3.2 ± 0.3 nm; stroma-side domains ~3.5 nm (PSI) and ~12 nm (ATP synthase); PSII only in appressed and PSI only in non-appressed membrane; "Cyt b₆f is present in both domains".
- **van Amerongen & Croce 2013** (full text): average Chl–Chl distance ≈ 10 Å.
- **FLV in cyanobacteria:** Helman et al. 2003 and Allahverdiyeva et al. 2013 (abstracts). FLV makes no reactive oxygen and is needed in fluctuating light; the cyanobacterial PGR5 is not crucial.
- **FLV in Chlamydomonas:** Chaux et al. 2017 (abstract). It drives most electron flow during induction.
- **FLV in moss:** Gerotto et al. 2016 (abstract). It is a safety valve.
- **FLV across plant groups:** Ilík et al. 2017 (abstract). It is active in all non-flowering green plant groups.
- **FLV put back into flowering plants:**
  - Yamamoto et al. 2016 (Arabidopsis; abstract). FLV protects in fluctuating light, and PGR5 is the main cyclic pathway.
  - Wada et al. 2018 (rice; abstract). FLV substitutes for cyclic flow without competing with CO₂ uptake.
  - Rizzetto et al. 2025 (tobacco; abstract). FLV competes with cyclic flow.
- **Why FLV was lost:** Alboresi et al. 2018 and Traverso et al. 2025 (abstracts).
- **O₂ outlet in flowering plants:** Badger et al. 2000 (abstract). The Mehler reaction carries probably less than 10 % of electron flow in C₃ and CAM plants.
- **Water–water cycle:** Asada 1999 (abstract).
- **Fossil thylakoids:** Demoulin et al. 2024 (abstract). Thylakoids are found in 1.78–1.73-billion-year-old fossils.
