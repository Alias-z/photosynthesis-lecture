## 1. The one idea under both lectures

**Leaves use light energy to take electrons from water and build sugar from CO₂.** Everything else is how that happens, how it is protected, and what it costs.

That is what the classic equation compresses:

> 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂

**Lecture 1 (Photo)** explains the left-hand half: light, water and O₂. Its output is ATP and NADPH.
**Lecture 2 (Synthesis)** explains the right-hand half: CO₂ and sugar. It spends that ATP and NADPH.

## 4. The essentials (what every student must leave with)

1. Light comes as photons. The number of photons counts, not their energy; a blue photon's extra energy becomes heat.
2. Pigments absorb selectively; leaves look green because green is absorbed least, yet they still use it.
3. Antennas collect, reaction centres convert: energy moves in the antenna, an electron moves at the reaction centre.
4. Water is the electron source, and all the O₂ comes from water.
5. Two photosystems in series lift electrons from water to NADP⁺ (the Z-scheme).
6. Electron flow builds a proton gradient; ATP synthase turns it into ATP.
7. Absorbed energy has competing fates. Fluorescence reports on them; triplets make singlet oxygen; NPQ protects; damage is repaired continuously.
8. Rubisco fixes CO₂ onto RuBP. Of every 6 triose phosphates, 5 rebuild RuBP and 1 is exported. 3 CO₂ need 9 ATP + 6 NADPH.
9. Rubisco also takes O₂. Photorespiration recovers 3 of every 4 carbons, at a cost.
10. C₂, C₄ and CAM concentrate CO₂ (in space or time) to suppress oxygenation, at an energy cost.
11. Leaf uptake is limited by the slower of Rubisco (CO₂) and RuBP regeneration (light); stomata gate CO₂ and cost water.
12. Sugar leaves the leaf as sucrose; starch carries the plant through the night; grain starch is rebuilt from sucrose.
