## Proposed order (variant 1, recommended)

The arc:
1. **The bridge:** how ATP and NADPH from lecture 1 reach carbon.
2. **The machine:** the cycle.
3. **Its output:** triose phosphate and where it goes.
4. **Its flaw:** Rubisco also takes O₂.
5. **What a whole leaf shows:** gas exchange and its limits.
6. **Evolution's fixes:** C₂, C₄, CAM.
7. **The future:** rising CO₂.
8. **The payoff:** bread.

**Why the whole-leaf part moves up:**
- FvCB is a C₃ model, and it follows directly from Rubisco and photorespiration (Γ\* is set by oxygenation).
- It shows that a C₃ leaf at today's CO₂ is limited by Rubisco. That is the problem C₂ and C₄ solve, and Γ and the A–Ci curve become the yardsticks by which their gains are measured.
- Stomata and water are in place before C₄'s water-use efficiency and CAM need them.
- All four reviewers reached it independently:
  - C and D proposed it.
  - A's dependency table requires the sugar-export slide before FvCB, which this order keeps.
  - B calls it the better order for Rubisco and photorespiration.

**What it costs:**
- About 6 minutes separate glycine decarboxylation (slide 7) from its move to the bundle sheath (slide 10). One recall line on slide 10 bridges it.
- The lecture no longer ends on FvCB's link to electron transport, so slide 14 carries that closure.
