# Light block: reviewed logic flow v5, 26 Oct 2026 (awaiting approval; nothing changed in the deck)

The goal is one chain of questions, each answered by one idea, keeping only the concepts the chain needs.

## The chain

| Step | Question | One idea that answers it | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Why is the sky blue, and why are leaves green? | (hook) | slide 2 lead page |
| 1 | Why is the sky blue? | **Light is a wave.** Sunlight mixes many wavelengths, and air scatters the short ones most. | sky animation → slide 3 |
| 2 | Does the wavelength matter to living things? | **Each living thing uses its own slice of light:** our eyes, a bee's eyes, a plant's chlorophyll. | life animation |
| 3 | What is physically different about each slice? | **Light is also a particle.** A photon's energy depends on its wavelength (Einstein), and the energy decides what a photon can do. | slide 4 |
| 4 | So why are leaves green? | **A pigment absorbs only photons whose energy fits it.** Chlorophyll takes blue and red, so green is left. | slide 5 |
| 5 | How does a leaf collect enough light? | Light harvesting | slide 6 |

Step 4 answers the hook's second half, which closes the loop.

## Each part, kept minimal

**Sky animation: 4 beats, each ≤ 2.5 s**

| # | Tag | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sunlight | Sunlight is a mix of wavelengths, from ultraviolet through visible light to infrared. |
| 2 | In the air | Air scatters short wavelengths far more than long ones: blue about four times more than red. *(Rayleigh clipping pops up.)* |
| 3 | Blue sky | Scattered blue reaches you from the whole sky, so the sky looks blue. |
| 4 | Sunset | At sunset, sunlight crosses far more air; most of the blue is scattered away, leaving red and orange. |

**Slide 3, light is a wave**
- A Rayleigh clipping with portrait, plus the spectrum strip with our vision range and PAR.
- Three points:
  1. Sunlight is a mix of wavelengths; we see ~380–780 nm, and PAR is 400–700 nm by convention.
  2. Air scatters short wavelengths most.
  3. At sunset the long path removes the blue.

**Life animation: 5 beats, each ≤ 2.5 s, one journey**

| # | Tag | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In the sea | Our ancestors lived in the sea. Water absorbs red light within a few metres; blue-green goes deepest. |
| 2 | On land | Most mammals see red and green as one colour. Primates evolved an extra colour receptor: ripe fruit stands out against the leaves. |
| 3 | Bees | Bees see ultraviolet but not red. Many flowers show them ultraviolet patterns that we cannot see. |
| 4 | Plants | Plants use light to make sugar. Their chlorophyll absorbs mostly blue and red. |
| 5 | So why? | Each living thing uses its own slice of light. **What is physically different about each slice?** |

How it looks:
- The fish crawls ashore and morphs along our lineage to a small mammal, then a monkey.
- Each eye is drawn with its light-sensitive cells as coloured dots: mammal 2, monkey 3, bee 3 including ultraviolet and no red. The leaf's chlorophyll glows where it catches blue and red.
- The bee flies in as its own animal, not a morph.

**Slide 4, light is also a particle**
- An Einstein clipping with portrait, plus a photon-energy ladder.
- Three points:
  1. Light comes in photons; E = hc/λ.
  2. The energy decides what a photon can do: ultraviolet can break bonds, visible can lift an electron in a pigment, infrared carries too little.
  3. Brighter light means more photons, not stronger ones.

**Slide 5, pigments: why leaves are green.** Its text is revised in its own round, to open with "a pigment absorbs only photons whose energy fits it".
