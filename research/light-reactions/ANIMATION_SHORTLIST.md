## Narrative before animation selection

First show today's overview of light and carbon reactions. Then open the light section with **“Why is the sky blue, and why are leaves green?”** The sky question motivates wavelength, energy and PAR; the existing animation explains the sky using those concepts. Return explicitly to the leaf question to introduce selective absorption and pigments. Only then ask what an absorbed photon does to chlorophyll, leading to excitation and photochemistry.

The existing animation was checked step by step in the browser. Its seven beats are: sunlight's colour mixture and short/long waves; scattering by air; scattered light reaching the observer; blue sky versus direct sunlight; the longer sunset path; preferential removal of shorter wavelengths; red transmitted sunlight. Preserve that progression as part of the explanation. PAR is the connection to plant light use, not a cause of the sky's colour.

Sequences 1–3 below continue this question-led progression. Their placement follows the concept they explain, rather than treating all existing pages as a generic opening block. See the revised [lecture outline](../../LECTURE_OUTLINE.md).

**Highest priority:** make the connection from electron flow to proton storage to ATP synthesis visible. **One historical experiment to develop:** Jagendorf and Uribe's ATP formation in darkness, as a short chapter within that sequence.

## Sequence 1 — one photon, several possible outcomes

Use three stops:

1. Absorb a photon and create Chl*. Separate photon energy from photon number.
2. Follow an **excitation marker** through the antenna to a reaction centre, then show **charge separation** with a different symbol.
3. Reveal the competing fluorescence and heat pathways. Keep these same pathways for the later NPQ sequence.

Ask: **“Does the electron travel through the antenna, or does excitation energy move?”**

Limit the display to a small antenna and one reaction centre. The different pigments can already have been introduced on a static absorption slide. Scientist names can be brief credits rather than separate historical episodes. [Structural/light-harvesting overview](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-biophys-021424-011156).

## Sequence 2 — the central mechanism, with one memorable experiment

Build it in chapters and stop after each. Preserve lumen/stroma orientation and the same electron symbols throughout.

1. **Water → NADPH:** trace four electrons through PSII, PQ, b6f, PC, PSI, Fd, and FNR. Show two light-driven boosts. The redox-energy view is a second view of those same electrons, not a second unrelated pathway.
2. **Proton accumulation → ATP:** reveal the proton movements after the electron route is understood. Use the four-electron budget already in the notes: one O₂, two NADPH, and the explicitly assumed ATP coupling. Pause the electron movement while explaining proton return through ATP synthase.
3. **The historical test, 60–90 seconds within the eight minutes:** impose a pH difference on a chloroplast preparation, switch the scene to darkness, and allow a transient ATP burst. This is the Jagendorf–Uribe acid–base experiment, not an intact leaf making ATP indefinitely at night.

Ask: **“If the lamp is off, what powers ATP formation here?”**

This experiment is worth the time because it separates the ultimate energy source from the immediate driving force. It also gives the proton gradient a testable function rather than leaving it as another arrow to memorize. [Jagendorf & Uribe (1966)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.55.1.170).

Use progressive counters, not simultaneous moving counters for every chemical species. During the first pass track electrons and NADPH; during the second, H⁺ and ATP. Finish on one stationary budget students can copy. A simple mechanical turn is enough for the ATP synthase; a separate actin-filament motor-history story is unnecessary here.

## Sequence 3 — reroute and protect, using the same scene

Split the four minutes across the existing cyclic-flow and photoprotection blocks:

1. Return an electron from the PSI acceptor side toward PQ. Show proton-coupled passage through b6f and **no additional net NADPH** from that circuit. Give the outcome before naming additional proteins.
2. Increase the heat-dissipation branch from sequence 1 as lumen acidification engages PsbS and xanthophyll-associated regulation. Show violaxanthin ↔ zeaxanthin as a small inset, not a second complete biochemical pathway.

Ask: **“Which output changes, and where can excess excitation go?”**

The story is **flexible routing and protection**, rather than a universal ATP shortage that must always be filled by one fixed cyclic fraction. Both linear and cyclic flow can contribute to the gradient; do not imply cyclic flow is the only trigger of NPQ. Precise PGR5-dependent Fd→PQ chemistry and the pigment-level quenching mechanism remain unresolved details. [2026 ATP measurements](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-026-02374-w), [NPQ evidence review](https://doi.org/10.1093/plcell/koaf240).
