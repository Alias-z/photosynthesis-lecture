# Animation scripts, slides 8–10b (draft for the lecturer, 27 Oct 2026, night)

Same frame as slide 7's animation:
- It opens and ends on the slide's own chain figure.
- The camera zooms into the focus component, shows what happens, then zooms back out.
- One click per beat; brisk moves of ≈ 1 s.
- Two stagings each: A on the c2c3 look (glow), B on the c2c look (bilayer).
- Each animation ends on the question the next slide answers.

## Slide 8 · Cytochrome b₆f (3 clicks, ≈ 25 s)
| Beat | Click plays | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Hand-over | zoom into cytochrome b₆f; plastoquinone (2 e⁻, 2 H⁺) docks; its 2 H⁺ drop into the lumen; the electrons enter b₆f | Plastoquinone docks at cytochrome b₆f and hands over its two electrons; its two H⁺ are released into the lumen. |
| 2 Plastocyanin | one electron passes to plastocyanin on the lumen side, which moves off along the lumen | Cytochrome b₆f passes the electrons one at a time to plastocyanin, a small copper protein that carries each through the lumen. |
| 3 H⁺ in the lumen | camera out to the whole chain; the H⁺ in the lumen add up; photosystem I starts to light | With every plastoquinone, more H⁺ collect in the lumen. **Where does plastocyanin take the electron?** |

## Slide 9 · Photosystem I (4 clicks, ≈ 35 s)
| Beat | Click plays | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Second light reaction | zoom into photosystem I; plastocyanin docks on the lumen side; light arrives; an electron crosses to ferredoxin | Light at photosystem I sends an electron across the membrane to ferredoxin; plastocyanin refills the reaction centre. |
| 2 NADPH | ferredoxin → FNR; NADP⁺ + H⁺ → NADPH in the stroma | Ferredoxin passes the electrons to FNR, which makes NADPH from NADP⁺ + H⁺ in the stroma. |
| 3 Emerson's enhancement | card: Emerson halftone portrait + his 1957 graph (O₂ per photon drops beyond 680 nm; recovers with added shorter red light) | 1957, Emerson: far-red light (beyond 680 nm) alone gave little O₂ per photon; added to shorter red light, it gave more than the two separately. |
| 4 Two spectra | back on the chain: a 650 nm photon goes to photosystem II, a far-red photon to photosystem I; zoom out; ATP synthase starts to light | Photosystem II, rich in Chl *b*, absorbs more at ≈ 470 and 650 nm; photosystem I also absorbs far-red. Both are needed in series. **What else does the chain make besides NADPH?** |

## Slide 10 · ATP synthase (4 clicks, ≈ 35 s)
| Beat | Click plays | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Proton gradient | whole chain: H⁺ from water and from cytochrome b₆f gather in the lumen, whose tint deepens | H⁺ from water splitting and from cytochrome b₆f collect in the lumen: it becomes more acidic than the stroma. |
| 2 ATP in the dark | card: Jagendorf & Uribe 1966 (their table as a clipping): chloroplasts in acid, moved to a basic buffer with ADP, make ATP in the dark | 1966, Jagendorf and Uribe soaked chloroplasts in acid, then moved them to a basic buffer with ADP: they made ATP in the dark. The H⁺ gradient alone drives ATP synthesis. |
| 3 The rotor | zoom into ATP synthase; H⁺ pass through its membrane part to the stroma; the rotor turns; ADP + Pᵢ → ATP at the head | H⁺ flow back to the stroma through ATP synthase; the flow turns its rotor, and its head makes ATP from ADP + Pᵢ. |
| 4 NADPH and ATP | whole chain; NADPH and ATP in the stroma | Light has made NADPH and ATP in the stroma. **Does the chain make them in the right ratio?** |

## Slide 10b · Cyclic electron flow (4 clicks, ≈ 35 s)
| Beat | Click plays | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 1 The gap | whole chain with a small readout: linear flow 2.6 ATP : 2 NADPH beside "CO₂ fixation needs 3 : 2" | Linear flow makes ≈ 2.6 ATP per 2 NADPH, but fixing CO₂ needs 3 ATP per 2 NADPH. |
| 2 The cycle | zoom on photosystem I and ferredoxin; the electron turns back to plastoquinone instead of FNR, and loops through b₆f, plastocyanin and photosystem I; H⁺ go into the lumen; FNR stays dim | In cyclic flow, ferredoxin returns electrons to plastoquinone. They cycle through cytochrome b₆f and photosystem I, moving more H⁺ into the lumen. |
| 3 Without it | card: Munekage et al. 2004, photos of *Arabidopsis* wild type vs plants lacking both cyclic routes | 2004: *Arabidopsis* plants lacking both routes of cyclic flow grow poorly. |
| 4 Extra ATP | zoom out; ATP synthase turns; the ATP readout rises to 3 : 2, NADPH unchanged | The extra H⁺ make extra ATP without NADPH, closing the gap. **What happens when light brings more energy than the chain can use?** |
