Headings state the fact (style A, lecturer's choice). The lines below give the evidence, numbers or definitions, without repeating the heading. Leads of 1–5 unchanged; leads of 6–14 trimmed where a heading now says the same.

| # | Lead | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy. | **Light reactions turn light energy into ATP and NADPH.** Pigments in the thylakoid membranes absorb the light / Electrons come from water; O₂ is released | **The Calvin–Benson cycle uses ATP and NADPH to reduce CO₂ to sugar.** Rubisco fixes CO₂ in the stroma / The carbon in bread comes from air | **Overall, CO₂ and water become sugar and O₂.** 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ |
| 2 | Light that meets matter is reflected, transmitted, absorbed or scattered, depending on its wavelength. | **Air scatters blue most, so the sky looks blue.** Rayleigh scattering by air molecules / Scattered blue reaches you from the whole sky | **Chlorophyll absorbs blue and red, so leaves look green.** Less green is absorbed, so more is reflected and transmitted | **Scattering keeps a photon's energy; absorption transfers it.** Only absorbed light can drive photosynthesis |
| 3 | Light is an electromagnetic wave; its wavelength (*λ*) sets its colour. | **Sunlight spans ultraviolet, visible and infrared.** Ultraviolet < 380 nm, infrared > 780 nm | **Plants use 400–700 nm (PAR).** Photosynthetically active radiation, by convention / Human vision: 380–780 nm | **Scattering rises as 1/*λ*⁴.** Blue is scattered ~4× more than red / Blue sky by day, red Sun at sunset |
| 4 | Light comes in discrete packets of energy: photons. | **Shorter wavelength, more energy per photon: *E* = *hc*/*λ*.** *h*: Planck constant · *c*: speed of light / A blue photon carries ~1.4× the energy of a red one | **UV breaks bonds, visible drives photochemistry, infrared heats.** UV: bonds in DNA and proteins break or rearrange / Infrared and microwaves only make molecules vibrate | **Light for photosynthesis is measured as photon flux.** Photosynthetic photon flux density (PPFD), µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ / Full sun ≈ 2000 · office ≈ 10 |
| 4a | A pigment absorbs a photon only if the photon's energy matches a gap between two electron energy levels. | **Red reaches S₁, blue a higher level; green matches only weak transitions.** Chl a absorbs strongly at ≈ 430 nm and ≈ 662 nm / Green falls between the two bands, so it is absorbed less | **Higher states relax to S₁ within 1 ps, releasing heat.** Internal conversion: the excess energy becomes molecular vibration | **Every absorbed photon yields one S₁ excitation, whatever its colour.** A blue photon (430 nm) loses ≈ 1/3 of its energy as heat on the way to S₁ |
| 4b | A pigment's colour comes from its structure: which electron jumps it has. | **The longer the conjugated chain, the longer the wavelength absorbed.** Conjugated: alternating single and double bonds share their electrons / 3 double bonds absorb UV (phytoene, ≈ 286 nm); 11 absorb blue (β-carotene, ≈ 450 nm) | **Chl b and carotenoids absorb where Chl a absorbs little.** Chl b: ≈ 453 and 642 nm / Carotenoids: 400–500 nm; they pass the energy to chlorophyll | **Leaves absorb 50–90 % of green and use it in deeper cells.** Green penetrates deeper into the leaf than blue or red (Terashima 2009) |
| 5 | Light is captured inside chloroplasts, by pigments held in their internal membranes. | **Each mesophyll cell holds many chloroplasts, ≈ 5 µm across.** Mesophyll: the inner cells of the leaf / Two envelope membranes wrap each chloroplast | **Thylakoids form stacked grana joined by stroma lamellae.** Grana 0.3–0.6 µm across / Around them is the stroma, where CO₂ is fixed (lecture 2) | **Proteins in the thylakoid membrane hold the pigments.** Pigment–protein complexes, e.g. the LHCII trimer, ≈ 8 nm across / The protein fixes each pigment's position and spacing |
| 6 | An antenna of light-harvesting complexes feeds each reaction centre. | **Excitation energy passes between chlorophylls 1–2 nm apart.** Only the energy moves; the electrons stay in place / Chl b → Chl a in ~1 ps, downhill in energy | **A reaction centre traps the energy, in PSII within ~150 ps.** Among Chl a the energy moves back and forth until it is trapped | **PSII uses LHCII; PSI uses LHCI with far-red chlorophylls.** Far-red chlorophylls emit at ≈ 730 nm / PSI traps in ~50 ps |
| 7 | In the reaction centre, light energy is stored as separated charge. | **Excited P680 gives an electron to pheophytin within ~3 ps.** P680: a pair of Chl a in the PSII reaction centre | **Transfer to Q_A and Q_B stabilises the separated charges.** Pheophytin → Q_A in ~250 ps → Q_B in 0.2–0.7 ms / Each step gives up a little energy, so the electron rarely returns | **P680⁺ oxidises water; reduced Q_B leaves as plastoquinol.** P680⁺ ≈ +1.25 V, one of the strongest oxidants in biology / Q_B takes two electrons and two H⁺ from the stroma |
| 8 | The Mn₄CaO₅ cluster of PSII refills P680⁺ with electrons from water. | **Each charge separation advances the Mn₄CaO₅ cluster by one S-state.** S₀ → S₁ → S₂ → S₃ → S₄ (Kok cycle) / At S₄: 2 H₂O → O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻ | **O₂ appears on every fourth flash, first on flash 3.** The cluster rests in S₁ in the dark / Measured by Joliot (1969), explained by Kok (1970) | **Structures show the cluster and the O–O bond forming.** 4 Mn, 1 Ca, 5 O at 1.9 Å (Umena 2011) / X-ray laser snapshots of the final step (Bhowmick 2023) |
| 9 | Electrons travel from water (+0.82 V) to NADP⁺ (−0.32 V) through PSII and PSI in series: the Z-scheme. | **Far-red light (> 685 nm) works poorly unless shorter light is added.** Emerson enhancement effect (1957) / Evidence for two photosystems with different pigments | **Plastoquinol, cytochrome b₆f and plastocyanin carry electrons from PSII to PSI.** PSI then reduces ferredoxin, which reduces NADP⁺ to NADPH | **PSII sits in the grana, PSI in the stroma lamellae.** Mapped in native thylakoids by cryo-electron tomography (Wietrzynski 2020) / Mobile plastoquinone and plastocyanin cover the distance |
| 10 | Electron flow loads the lumen with protons; flowing back through ATP synthase, they make ATP in the stroma. | **Linear flow moves 12 H⁺ into the lumen per O₂.** 4 from water, 8 moved by cytochrome b₆f (Q cycle) | **14 H⁺ turn ATP synthase once and make 3 ATP.** The c-ring has 14 subunits / 4.67 H⁺ per ATP | **A pH gradient alone drives ATP synthesis.** In the dark, thylakoids moved from pH 3.8 to pH 8 made ATP (Jagendorf & Uribe 1966) / Evidence for Mitchell's chemiosmotic theory |
| 11 | The Calvin–Benson cycle uses 3 ATP per 2 NADPH; the chloroplast adjusts its electron flow to match. | **Linear flow gives ≈ 2.6 ATP per 2 NADPH, short of the 3 needed.** 12 H⁺ ÷ 4.67 H⁺ per ATP ≈ 2.6 | **Cyclic flow around PSI makes ATP without NADPH.** Electrons from PSI return to plastoquinone (via PGR5 or NDH) / Plants lacking both routes stay small (Munekage 2004) | **State transitions move LHCII from PSII to PSI.** A reduced plastoquinone pool activates the STN7 kinase, which phosphorylates LHCII / This balances excitation between the photosystems |
| 12 | An excited chlorophyll loses its energy by photochemistry, heat or fluorescence. | **The yields of photochemistry, heat and fluorescence add up to 1.** Each share = its rate ÷ the sum of all rates / When one pathway slows, the others gain | **An open PSII traps the excitation before heat or fluorescence can.** Open: trapping in ≈ 0.3 ns / Closed (Q_A reduced): the excitation lasts ≈ 2 ns; heat and fluorescence rise | **A long-lived excitation can form a triplet and then singlet O₂.** Singlet O₂ damages proteins and lipids / Carotenoids quench triplets in the antenna, but not at P680 |
| 13 | In excess light, leaves dissipate energy as heat and repair damaged PSII. | **Low lumen pH activates PsbS and zeaxanthin, which dissipate energy as heat.** Non-photochemical quenching (NPQ) / Without PsbS, NPQ falls from ≈ 2.4 to ≈ 0.5 (Li 2000) | **PSII damage rises with light and is repaired by replacing D1.** Damage rate ∝ light (Tyystjärvi & Aro 1996) / D1: the reaction-centre protein most often damaged | **NPQ relaxes slowly after shading, which costs CO₂ fixation.** Faster relaxation raised field yield: tobacco +14–20 %, soybean up to +33 % (one of two years) / Arabidopsis and rice grew worse |
| 14 | Fluorescence competes with photochemistry and heat, so its changes report on both. | **A saturating pulse in darkness gives the maximum PSII efficiency, Fv/Fm.** The pulse closes every PSII: Fo (open) → Fm (closed) / Fv/Fm = (Fm − Fo)/Fm ≈ 0.83 in healthy leaves | **In light the pulse gives ΦPSII and NPQ.** ΦPSII = (Fm′ − Fs)/Fm′ / NPQ = Fm/Fm′ − 1 | **Satellites measure the same fluorescence over whole crops.** Solar-induced fluorescence (SIF) maps crop photosynthesis (Guanter 2014) / ESA's FLEX satellite launched 15 Sep 2026 |
