Every line names what moves, where, and how much. No evaluative phrases ("too little", "regulates", "most").

| # | Title | Lead | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Light harvesting | Antenna complexes collect light and pass the energy to the reaction centres. | **Antenna.** ≈ 200–300 chlorophylls per PSII reaction centre / One chlorophyll absorbs ~10 photons per second in full sun | **Energy transfer.** Carotenoids, Chl b (≈ 650 nm) → Chl a (≈ 670–680 nm) → P680 / Each step goes to a pigment of lower excitation energy | **PSII and PSI antennas.** PSII: LHCII trimers (Chl a, Chl b, carotenoids) + core antenna CP43, CP47 / PSI: LHCI, with Chl a absorbing beyond 700 nm |
| 7 | Photosystem II | PSII uses light energy to move electrons from water to plastoquinone. | **Charge separation.** P680* → pheophytin → Q_A → Q_B / The electron crosses from the lumen side to the stroma side | **Water oxidation.** Mn₄CaO₅ cluster, lumen side: 2 H₂O → O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻ / O₂ leaves the leaf; the 4 H⁺ stay in the lumen | **Plastoquinone reduction.** Q_B + 2 e⁻ + 2 H⁺ (from the stroma) → plastoquinol (PQH₂) / PQH₂ diffuses in the membrane to cytochrome b₆f |
| 8 | Electron transport to NADPH | Electrons flow from PSII through cytochrome b₆f and PSI to NADP⁺: the Z-scheme. | **Cytochrome b₆f.** PQH₂ → b₆f → plastocyanin (lumen) / Per 2 electrons, 4 H⁺ enter the lumen (Q cycle) | **Photosystem I.** Plastocyanin → P700 → ferredoxin (stroma) / Ferredoxin–NADP⁺ reductase: 2 ferredoxin⁻ + NADP⁺ + H⁺ → NADPH | **Z-scheme.** Water (+0.82 V) → PSII → b₆f → PSI → NADP⁺ (−0.32 V) / 8 photons move 4 e⁻ from 2 H₂O to 2 NADPH |
| 9 | ATP synthesis | H⁺ accumulated in the lumen flow back through ATP synthase and drive ATP synthesis. | **Proton gradient.** Per O₂: 4 H⁺ from water + 8 H⁺ from b₆f = 12 H⁺ / In light: lumen ≈ pH 6, stroma ≈ pH 8 | **ATP synthase.** CF₀ in the membrane: 14 H⁺ turn the c-ring once / CF₁ in the stroma: 3 ADP + 3 Pᵢ → 3 ATP per turn | **Chemiosmosis.** Mitchell 1961: a proton gradient couples electron transport to ATP synthesis / Jagendorf 1966: thylakoids moved from pH 4 to pH 8 make ATP in the dark |
| 10 | ATP and NADPH supply | The Calvin–Benson cycle uses 3 ATP : 2 NADPH. | **Linear electron flow.** Water → PSII → PSI → NADPH / 2.6 ATP : 2 NADPH | **Cyclic electron flow.** PSI → ferredoxin → plastoquinone → b₆f → PSI / ATP only, no NADPH | **State transitions.** Reduced plastoquinone → LHCII phosphorylated / Phosphorylated LHCII moves from PSII to PSI |
| 11 | Excess light | In full sun, leaves absorb more light than photosynthesis can use. | **Light saturation.** C₃ leaves: CO₂ fixation saturates at ≈ 500–1000 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ / Full sun: ≈ 2000 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ | **Fates of absorbed energy.** Photochemistry + heat + fluorescence = 1 / Low light: ≈ 80 % photochemistry; full sun: mostly heat | **Reactive oxygen species.** PSII: triplet chlorophyll + O₂ → singlet O₂ / PSI: electrons + O₂ → superoxide / Both oxidise D1, lipids and pigments |
| 12 | Photoprotection and repair | Leaves dissipate excess energy as heat and repair damaged PSII. | **Non-photochemical quenching (NPQ).** Low lumen pH protonates PsbS and activates violaxanthin → zeaxanthin / LHCII then converts excitation into heat | **Photoinhibition and repair.** D1 of PSII is damaged at a rate proportional to light / Damaged D1 is degraded and a new copy inserted | **NPQ and crop yield.** After shading, NPQ relaxes over minutes while CO₂ fixation stays low / Faster relaxation (VPZ): tobacco +14–20 % dry weight (Kromdijk 2016), soybean up to +33 % seed yield (De Souza 2022) |
| 13 | Chlorophyll fluorescence | Fluorescence competes with photochemistry and heat, so it reports on both. | **Fv/Fm.** Dark-adapted leaf, saturating pulse: Fo → Fm / Fv/Fm = (Fm − Fo)/Fm: ≈ 0.83 healthy, lower when stressed | **ΦPSII and NPQ.** In light: ΦPSII = (Fm′ − Fs)/Fm′ / NPQ = Fm/Fm′ − 1 | **Solar-induced fluorescence (SIF).** Emitted at ≈ 685 and 740 nm, measured from satellites / Maps crop photosynthesis (Guanter 2014) |

To verify before landing: ≈ 200–300 Chl per PSII; ~10 photons s⁻¹ per Chl; Chl b ≈ 650 nm, Chl a ≈ 670–680 nm in vivo; lumen ≈ pH 6, stroma ≈ pH 8; C₃ saturation ≈ 500–1000 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹; ≈ 80 % photochemistry in low light; Jagendorf pH 3.8 → 8 (written 4 → 8).
