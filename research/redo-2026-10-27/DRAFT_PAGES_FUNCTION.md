# Slides 6–13 rethought for bachelor students: function and connections (27 Oct 2026, for approval; nothing changed)

Thread: light → antenna → PSII → electrons to NADPH and H⁺ into the lumen → ATP → balance → excess light → protection → measurement. The acidic lumen links 7 → 8 → 9 → 12. Charge separation and water oxidation are merged into one PSII slide (one functional unit), so 6–14 becomes 6–13.

| # | Title | Lead | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Light harvesting | Antenna complexes collect light and pass the energy to the reaction centres. | **Antenna.** Hundreds of pigments serve one reaction centre / A single chlorophyll absorbs only ~10 photons per second in full sun | **Energy transfer.** From pigment to pigment, ending at the reaction centre / Most absorbed photons (≈ 85–90 % in PSII) reach a reaction centre | **PSII and PSI antennas.** PSII with LHCII, PSI with LHCI / LHCII also regulates light capture (slides 10, 12) |
| 7 | Photosystem II | PSII uses light to move electrons from water to plastoquinone. | **Charge separation.** The excited reaction-centre chlorophyll (P680) passes an electron across the membrane / Light energy is now stored as separated charge | **Water oxidation.** P680⁺ takes electrons from water at the Mn₄CaO₅ cluster / 2 H₂O → O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻: O₂ is released, the H⁺ stay in the lumen | **Plastoquinone reduction.** On the stroma side, plastoquinone takes 2 e⁻ and 2 H⁺ and becomes plastoquinol (PQH₂) / PQH₂ carries them through the membrane to cytochrome b₆f |
| 8 | Electron transport to NADPH | Electrons flow from PSII through cytochrome b₆f and PSI to NADP⁺: the Z-scheme. | **Cytochrome b₆f.** Oxidises PQH₂ and releases its H⁺ into the lumen / The Q cycle doubles the H⁺ moved per electron / The slowest step of the chain | **Photosystem I.** Plastocyanin brings the electrons to PSI; light lifts them a second time / PSI reduces ferredoxin, which reduces NADP⁺ to NADPH in the stroma | **Z-scheme.** Two light-driven steps in series / 8 photons move 4 electrons from 2 H₂O to 2 NADPH |
| 9 | ATP synthesis | The H⁺ gathered in the lumen flow back through ATP synthase and drive ATP synthesis. | **Lumen acidification.** 12 H⁺ per O₂: 4 from water, 8 via cytochrome b₆f / In light: lumen ≈ pH 6, stroma ≈ pH 8 | **ATP synthase.** H⁺ flowing back to the stroma turn its rotor / 14 H⁺ per 3 ATP; ATP is released into the stroma | **Chemiosmosis.** The proton gradient couples electron transport to ATP synthesis (Mitchell) / An acid-to-base jump alone makes thylakoids produce ATP in the dark (Jagendorf 1966) |
| 10 | Matching ATP and NADPH | The Calvin–Benson cycle needs 3 ATP per 2 NADPH; electron transport adjusts to supply this ratio. | **Linear electron flow.** ≈ 2.6 ATP per 2 NADPH: too little ATP | **Cyclic electron flow.** Electrons from PSI return to plastoquinone / More H⁺ and ATP, no NADPH | **State transitions.** When PSII is over-excited, part of LHCII moves to PSI / Balances the two photosystems |
| 11 | Excess light | In full sun, leaves absorb more light than photosynthesis can use. | **Light saturation.** CO₂ fixation saturates well below full sun / Absorption keeps rising with light | **Fates of absorbed energy.** Photochemistry, heat and fluorescence compete / When photochemistry is saturated, heat and fluorescence rise | **Reactive oxygen species.** Excess excitation forms singlet O₂ at PSII / Excess electrons reduce O₂ at PSI / Both damage proteins and membranes |
| 12 | Photoprotection and repair | Leaves dissipate excess energy as heat and repair damaged PSII. | **Non-photochemical quenching (NPQ).** The acidic lumen signals excess light / It activates PsbS and zeaxanthin; the antenna then dissipates energy as heat | **Photoinhibition and repair.** PSII is damaged in proportion to light / Its D1 protein is replaced continuously | **NPQ and crop yield.** NPQ relaxes slowly after shading and lowers CO₂ fixation / Faster relaxation raised field yield in tobacco and soybean (Kromdijk 2016; De Souza 2022) |
| 13 | Chlorophyll fluorescence | Fluorescence competes with photochemistry and heat, so it reports on both. | **Fv/Fm.** Measured in darkness with a saturating pulse / ≈ 0.83 in healthy leaves; lower values indicate stress | **ΦPSII and NPQ.** Measured in light / The share of light used by PSII, and the heat dissipation | **Solar-induced fluorescence (SIF).** Measured by satellites / Tracks crop photosynthesis over whole regions |
