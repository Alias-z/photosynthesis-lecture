Headings as on slides 1–4: the textbook term, with a short key fact where it helps ("Light intensity = photon flux"). Facts and numbers sit in the lines. Slides 1–5 keep their live headings; 4a and 4b change.

| 4a | A pigment absorbs a photon only if the photon's energy matches a gap between two electron energy levels. | **Absorption bands: red and blue.** Red lifts an electron to S₁, blue to a higher level (Sₙ) / Green falls between the two bands; only weak transitions match it, so it is absorbed less | **Internal conversion to S₁.** Higher states relax to S₁ within 1 ps; the excess energy becomes heat | **One S₁ excitation per photon.** The colour sets only how much energy is lost as heat / Blue (430 nm): ≈ 1/3 of its energy |
| 4b | A pigment's colour comes from its structure: which electron jumps it has. | **Conjugation: longer chain, longer wavelength.** Alternating single and double bonds share their electrons / 3 double bonds absorb UV (phytoene, ≈ 286 nm); 11 absorb blue (β-carotene, ≈ 450 nm) | **Accessory pigments: Chl b and carotenoids.** They absorb where Chl a absorbs little: Chl b at ≈ 453 and 642 nm, carotenoids at 400–500 nm / They pass the energy to Chl a | **Green light: absorbed deeper in the leaf.** Leaves absorb 50–90 % of green / It penetrates further than blue or red and drives photosynthesis in deeper cells (Terashima 2009) |
| 6 | An antenna of light-harvesting complexes feeds each reaction centre. | **Excitation energy transfer.** Energy, not an electron, passes between chlorophylls 1–2 nm apart / Chl b → Chl a in ~1 ps, downhill in energy | **Trapping at the reaction centre.** Among Chl a the energy moves back and forth until a reaction centre traps it / PSII: ~150 ps after absorption | **PSII and PSI antennas.** PSII: LHCII / PSI: LHCI with far-red chlorophylls (emission ≈ 730 nm); trapping in ~50 ps |
| 7 | In the reaction centre, light energy is stored as separated charge. | **Primary charge separation: ~3 ps.** Excited P680, a pair of Chl a, gives an electron to pheophytin | **Charge stabilisation.** Pheophytin → Q_A in ~250 ps → Q_B in 0.2–0.7 ms / Each step gives up a little energy, so the electron rarely returns | **P680⁺ and plastoquinol.** P680⁺ (≈ +1.25 V) takes electrons from water (slide 8) / Q_B takes two electrons and two H⁺, then leaves as plastoquinol (slide 9) |
| 8 | The Mn₄CaO₅ cluster of PSII refills P680⁺ with electrons from water. | **Kok cycle: one S-state per photon.** Each charge separation removes one electron from the cluster: S₀ → S₄ / At S₄: 2 H₂O → O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻ | **Flash O₂ yield: a period of four.** Dark-adapted thylakoids release O₂ mainly on flashes 3, 7, 11 (Joliot 1969; Kok 1970) / The cluster rests in S₁ in the dark | **Structure of the Mn₄CaO₅ cluster.** 4 Mn, 1 Ca, 5 O at 1.9 Å (Umena 2011) / X-ray laser snapshots catch the O–O bond forming (Bhowmick 2023) |
| 9 | Electrons travel from water (+0.82 V) to NADP⁺ (−0.32 V) through PSII and PSI in series: the Z-scheme. | **Emerson enhancement effect.** Light beyond 685 nm alone releases O₂ poorly / Adding shorter-wavelength light restores it: evidence for two photosystems | **Electron carriers between PSII and PSI.** Plastoquinol → cytochrome b₆f → plastocyanin → PSI / PSI reduces ferredoxin, which reduces NADP⁺ to NADPH | **Lateral heterogeneity: PSII in grana, PSI in stroma lamellae.** Mapped in native thylakoids by cryo-electron tomography (Wietrzynski 2020) / Mobile plastoquinone and plastocyanin cover the distance |
| 10 | Electron flow loads the lumen with protons; flowing back through ATP synthase, they make ATP in the stroma. | **Proton sources.** Per O₂: 4 H⁺ from water, 8 moved by cytochrome b₆f (Q cycle) | **ATP synthase.** 14 H⁺ turn its c-ring (14 subunits) once: 3 ATP / 4.67 H⁺ per ATP | **Jagendorf acid-bath experiment.** In the dark, thylakoids moved from pH 3.8 to pH 8 made ATP (1966) / A pH gradient alone drives ATP synthesis: evidence for Mitchell's chemiosmotic theory |
| 11 | The light reactions must supply ATP and NADPH in the ratio the Calvin–Benson cycle uses. | **ATP : NADPH ratio.** Linear flow: 12 H⁺ ÷ 4.67 ≈ 2.6 ATP per 2 NADPH / The Calvin–Benson cycle needs 3 | **Cyclic electron flow.** Electrons from PSI return to plastoquinone (via PGR5 or NDH): ATP without NADPH / Plants lacking both routes stay small (Munekage 2004) | **State transitions.** A reduced plastoquinone pool activates the STN7 kinase / Phosphorylated LHCII moves from PSII to PSI |
| 12 | An excited chlorophyll loses its energy by photochemistry, heat or fluorescence. | **Competing pathways: yields add up to 1.** Each pathway's share = its rate ÷ the sum of all rates / When one slows, the others gain | **Open and closed reaction centres.** Open PSII traps the excitation in ≈ 0.3 ns / Closed (Q_A reduced): it lasts ≈ 2 ns; heat and fluorescence rise | **Triplets and singlet oxygen.** A long-lived excitation can form a triplet, which makes singlet O₂ / Carotenoids quench triplets in the antenna, but not at P680 |
| 13 | In excess light, leaves dissipate energy as heat and repair damaged PSII. | **Non-photochemical quenching (NPQ).** Low lumen pH activates PsbS and converts violaxanthin to zeaxanthin; the antenna dissipates energy as heat / Without PsbS, NPQ falls from ≈ 2.4 to ≈ 0.5 (Li 2000) | **Photoinhibition and D1 repair.** PSII damage rises in proportion to light (Tyystjärvi & Aro 1996) / The damaged D1 protein is replaced by a new copy | **NPQ relaxation and crop yield.** NPQ relaxes in minutes after shading, wasting light / Faster relaxation: tobacco +14–20 %, soybean up to +33 % (one of two years); Arabidopsis and rice grew worse |
| 14 | Fluorescence competes with photochemistry and heat, so its changes report on both. | **Saturating pulse: Fv/Fm.** A pulse closes every PSII: Fo (open) → Fm (closed) / Fv/Fm = (Fm − Fo)/Fm ≈ 0.83 in healthy dark-adapted leaves | **In light: ΦPSII and NPQ.** ΦPSII = (Fm′ − Fs)/Fm′ / NPQ = Fm/Fm′ − 1 | **Solar-induced fluorescence (SIF).** Satellites map crop photosynthesis from the same fluorescence (Guanter 2014) / ESA's FLEX satellite launched 15 Sep 2026 |
