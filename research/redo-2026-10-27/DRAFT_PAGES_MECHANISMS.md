# Slides 6–13: mechanisms students should remember (27 Oct 2026, for approval; nothing changed)

Lead = take-home. Each point = a mechanism in plain words. Numbers only where the number is the idea (3 ATP : 2 NADPH, Fv/Fm ≈ 0.8).

| # | Title | Lead (take-home) | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Light harvesting | Antenna pigments absorb light and pass the energy to a reaction centre, where it is used. | **Antenna.** Many pigments collect light for one reaction centre / So the reaction centre is supplied even in dim light | **Energy transfer.** Carotenoids and Chl b pass energy to Chl a; Chl a passes it to the reaction centre / Energy flows from higher- to lower-energy pigments, so it ends in the reaction centre | **PSII and PSI antennas.** Each photosystem has its own antenna: LHCII for PSII, LHCI for PSI / LHCII can move to PSI or turn excess energy into heat (slides 10, 12) |
| 7 | Photosystem II | PSII uses light to take electrons from water and pass them to plastoquinone; O₂ is released and the lumen is acidified. | **Charge separation.** The excited reaction-centre chlorophyll (P680) gives away an electron / The electron crosses the membrane: light energy is stored as separated charge | **Water oxidation.** P680⁺ takes electrons from water / Water is split into O₂, electrons and H⁺; the H⁺ stay in the lumen and acidify it | **Plastoquinone.** Plastoquinone takes up electrons and H⁺ from the stroma and becomes plastoquinol / Plastoquinol carries them through the membrane to cytochrome b₆f |
| 8 | Electron transport to NADPH | Electrons travel from PSII through cytochrome b₆f and PSI to NADP⁺; light energises them twice. | **Cytochrome b₆f.** Passes electrons from plastoquinol to plastocyanin / Releases H⁺ into the lumen and pumps extra H⁺ (Q cycle) | **Photosystem I.** Light energises the electrons a second time / PSI passes them via ferredoxin to NADP⁺, making NADPH in the stroma | **Z-scheme.** PSII makes an oxidant strong enough to split water / PSI makes a reductant strong enough to produce NADPH |
| 9 | ATP synthesis | The H⁺ gradient across the thylakoid membrane drives ATP synthase, which makes ATP in the stroma. | **Proton gradient.** Water splitting and cytochrome b₆f load the lumen with H⁺ / The acidic lumen stores the energy of electron transport | **ATP synthase.** H⁺ flow back to the stroma through ATP synthase and turn its rotor / The rotation drives ADP + Pᵢ → ATP | **Chemiosmosis.** Electron transport and ATP synthesis are linked only through the H⁺ gradient (Mitchell) / An artificial pH gradient makes thylakoids produce ATP in the dark (Jagendorf 1966) |
| 10 | ATP and NADPH supply | The Calvin–Benson cycle uses 3 ATP per 2 NADPH; chloroplasts adjust the supply by changing electron routes. | **Linear electron flow.** Water → PSII → PSI → NADP⁺ / Makes ATP and NADPH (≈ 2.6 ATP per 2 NADPH) | **Cyclic electron flow.** Electrons from PSI return to plastoquinone and cycle through b₆f / Pumps H⁺ and makes ATP, but no NADPH | **State transitions.** When PSII receives more light than PSI, LHCII moves from PSII to PSI / Both photosystems then work at a similar rate |
| 11 | Excess light | In bright light, leaves absorb more energy than they can use; the excess must be removed or it damages the photosystems. | **Light saturation.** CO₂ fixation reaches its maximum well below full sunlight / Light absorbed beyond that cannot be used | **Fates of absorbed energy.** Each excitation is used in photochemistry, lost as heat or re-emitted as fluorescence / When photochemistry is saturated, more goes to heat and fluorescence | **Reactive oxygen species.** Energy and electrons that cannot be used pass to O₂ / Singlet oxygen and superoxide damage proteins (especially D1), lipids and pigments |
| 12 | Photoprotection and repair | The acidic lumen signals excess light and switches on heat dissipation (NPQ); damaged PSII is repaired continuously. | **Non-photochemical quenching (NPQ).** The acidic lumen activates PsbS and converts violaxanthin to zeaxanthin / The antenna then turns excess energy into heat | **Photoinhibition and repair.** PSII, especially its D1 protein, is damaged in proportion to light / Damaged D1 is replaced; photoinhibition appears when damage outpaces repair | **NPQ and crop yield.** NPQ switches off slowly when light drops, so shaded leaves waste light for minutes / Faster NPQ relaxation increased field yield of tobacco and soybean |
| 13 | Chlorophyll fluorescence | Fluorescence competes with photochemistry and heat, so measuring it shows how a leaf uses light, without harming it. | **Fv/Fm.** Measured after dark adaptation with a saturating light pulse / Maximum PSII efficiency: ≈ 0.8 in healthy leaves, lower under stress | **ΦPSII and NPQ.** Measured in the light / ΦPSII: the share of absorbed light used by PSII; NPQ: the heat dissipation | **Solar-induced fluorescence (SIF).** Satellites detect the faint fluorescence of vegetation / It tracks crop photosynthesis across regions |
