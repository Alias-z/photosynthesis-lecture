| 6 | Light harvesting | Antenna pigments pass the absorbed energy, not an electron, from one to the next until a reaction centre traps it. | 1 **Energy hops, electrons stay.** Chlorophylls sit 1–2 nm apart / Chl b → Chl a in ~1 ps, downhill in energy<br>2 **The reaction centre ends the walk.** Among Chl a the energy moves back and forth / until a reaction centre traps it: PSII in ~150 ps<br>3 **Two antennas.** PSII: LHCII trimers / PSI: LHCI, with far-red chlorophylls (emission ≈ 730 nm); traps in ~50 ps |
| 7 | Charge separation | In the reaction centre, the excitation moves an electron away from P680: light energy is stored as separated charge. | 1 **An electron leaves in picoseconds.** Excited P680 gives an electron to pheophytin within ~3 ps<br>2 **Each step slower, the charges further apart.** Pheophytin → Q_A in ~250 ps → Q_B in 0.2–0.7 ms / Each step gives up a little energy, so the electron rarely returns<br>3 **Both ends now react.** P680⁺ (≈ +1.25 V) takes an electron from water (slide 8) / Q_B, after two electrons and two H⁺, leaves as plastoquinol (slide 9) |
| 8 | Water oxidation | The Mn₄CaO₅ cluster of PSII refills P680⁺ with electrons from water; every four release one O₂. | 1 **One photon, one step.** Each charge separation removes one electron from the cluster: S₀ → S₄ / At S₄: 2 H₂O → O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻<br>2 **Flashes reveal the cycle.** Dark-adapted thylakoids release O₂ mainly on flashes 3, 7, 11 (Joliot 1969; Kok 1970) / The cluster rests in S₁, so the first O₂ needs three flashes<br>3 **Seen at atomic scale.** 4 Mn, 1 Ca, 5 O (Umena 2011, 1.9 Å) / X-ray laser snapshots catch the O–O bond forming (Bhowmick 2023) |
| 9 | Two photosystems in series | Electrons travel from water (+0.82 V) to NADP⁺ (−0.32 V) through PSII and PSI in series, each adding a light-driven boost: the Z-scheme. | 1 **Two colours work better together.** Light beyond 685 nm alone releases O₂ poorly / Adding shorter-wavelength light restores it (Emerson enhancement, 1957)<br>2 **Carriers link the photosystems.** Plastoquinol → cytochrome b₆f → plastocyanin → PSI / PSI reduces ferredoxin, which reduces NADP⁺ to NADPH<br>3 **The photosystems sit apart.** PSII in the grana, PSI in the stroma lamellae / Mobile plastoquinone and plastocyanin cover the distance |
| 10 | A proton gradient drives ATP synthase | Electron flow loads the lumen with protons; flowing back through ATP synthase, they make ATP in the stroma. | 1 **12 H⁺ per O₂.** 4 from water, 8 moved by cytochrome b₆f (Q cycle)<br>2 **A rotary motor.** 14 H⁺ turn the 14-subunit c-ring once: 3 ATP / 4.67 H⁺ per ATP<br>3 **The gradient alone makes ATP.** Thylakoids moved from pH 3.8 to pH 8 make ATP in the dark (Jagendorf & Uribe 1966) / Evidence for Mitchell's chemiosmotic theory |
| 11 | Balancing ATP and NADPH | The Calvin–Benson cycle uses 3 ATP per 2 NADPH; linear electron flow alone gives too little ATP. | 1 **Linear flow: ≈ 2.6 ATP per 2 NADPH.** 12 H⁺ ÷ 4.67 H⁺ per ATP / Short of the 3 the cycle needs<br>2 **Cyclic flow adds ATP only.** Electrons from PSI return to plastoquinone (via PGR5 or NDH) / H⁺ are pumped, no NADPH is made; plants lacking both routes stay small<br>3 **State transitions balance the photosystems.** A reduced plastoquinone pool activates the STN7 kinase / Phosphorylated LHCII moves from PSII to PSI |
| 12 | Fates of an excitation | An excited chlorophyll loses its energy by photochemistry, heat or fluorescence, which compete for it. | 1 **Yields add up to 1.** Each exit's share = its rate ÷ the sum of all rates / When one exit slows, the others gain<br>2 **Open centres win.** An open PSII traps the excitation in ≈ 0.3 ns / With Q_A reduced (closed), it lingers ≈ 2 ns: heat and fluorescence rise<br>3 **Lingering is risky.** A lingering excitation can form a triplet, which makes singlet O₂ / Carotenoids quench triplets in the antenna, but not at P680 |
| 13 | Too much light: dissipation and repair | In excess light the antenna switches to dissipating energy as heat (NPQ, non-photochemical quenching), and damaged PSII is repaired. | 1 **An acidic lumen switches on NPQ.** Low lumen pH activates PsbS and converts violaxanthin to zeaxanthin / Without PsbS, NPQ falls from ≈ 2.4 to ≈ 0.5 (Li 2000)<br>2 **Damage grows with light; D1 is replaced.** PSII damage rises in proportion to light (Tyystjärvi & Aro 1996) / The damaged D1 protein is replaced by a new copy<br>3 **Slow relaxation costs carbon.** NPQ switches on in seconds but relaxes in minutes, wasting light after shading / Faster relaxation raised field yield in tobacco (+14–20 %) and soybean (up to +33 %, one year of two); Arabidopsis and rice grew worse |
| 14 | Reading photosynthesis by fluorescence | Fluorescence competes with photochemistry and heat, so its changes report on both. | 1 **A saturating pulse closes every PSII.** Fo: all open (dark) · Fm: all closed (pulse) / Fv/Fm = (Fm − Fo)/Fm ≈ 0.83 in healthy dark-adapted leaves<br>2 **In light: efficiency and heat.** ΦPSII = (Fm′ − Fs)/Fm′ / NPQ = Fm/Fm′ − 1<br>3 **From leaf to orbit.** Satellites map solar-induced fluorescence of crops (Guanter 2014) / ESA's FLEX satellite, launched 15 Sep 2026, is built for it |
