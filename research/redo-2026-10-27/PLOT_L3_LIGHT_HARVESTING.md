## Built only on what students already know
- Slide 4: light intensity = photon flux (full sun ≈ 2000, office ≈ 10).
- 4a: an absorbed photon leaves the pigment excited (S₁).
- 4b: several pigments (Chl a, Chl b, carotenoids) catch different colours.

## Concepts to introduce, in order
1. Light limitation in shade
2. Chloroplast movement
3. Grana stacking
4. Photosynthetic unit: antenna + reaction centre
5. Excitation energy transfer, downhill to the reaction centre

## The story: one zoom, from the forest floor to the reaction centre

| Step | Question | Answer / concept | Raises |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | How do plants on a dark forest floor collect enough light? | A few percent of full sun reaches the ground (slide 4) | What can a leaf change to catch more? |
| Cell | Can a plant rearrange itself? (Senn 1908) | **Chloroplast movement**: in dim light, chloroplasts gather on the cell walls that face the light | And inside each chloroplast? |
| Chloroplast | How much light-catching membrane? | **Grana stacking**: shade chloroplasts stack far more thylakoids | Do all those chlorophylls do the chemistry? |
| Membrane | Does every chlorophyll make O₂? (Emerson & Arnold 1932) | **Photosynthetic unit**: one O₂ per ~2,400 chlorophylls; most chlorophylls only collect light (antenna) for a few reaction centres | How does the energy of hundreds of collectors reach one centre, including what Chl b and carotenoids catch (4b)? |
| Pigments | How does the energy travel? | **Excitation energy transfer**: pigment to pigment, downhill in energy, from carotenoids and Chl b to Chl a, to the reaction centre, which traps it | End of the animation. Into chunk 4: what does the reaction centre do with it? |

## On the study page (draft text, for approval)
- Lead: Antenna pigments pass absorbed light energy from pigment to pigment to a reaction centre, where it is used.
- Photosynthetic unit: hundreds of pigments in light-harvesting complexes serve one reaction centre (Emerson & Arnold 1932).
- Excitation energy transfer: carotenoids, Chl b → Chl a → reaction centre; each hop goes downhill in energy.
- Shade acclimation: in dim light chloroplasts gather on the lit cell walls, stack more grana and build larger antennas.
