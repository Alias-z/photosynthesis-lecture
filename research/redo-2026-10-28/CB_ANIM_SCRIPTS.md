# Animation scripts, lecture 2 chunk 1: the Calvin–Benson–Bassham cycle (C1–C4), draft for the lecturer, 28 Oct 2026

Phase 1 only: scripts, nothing built. Each animation follows its slide, opens and ends on the slide's own figure where
it reuses it, takes ≈ 35 s in four clicks (moves ≈ 0.8–1.2 s; a click stop only where the camera moves to a new view;
cause and effect in one click), names every element, tells at most one history story, and ends on the question the
next slide answers. Captions use only facts from papers read for this chunk (sources under each table). Keys carry
the chunk prefix.

## cb-cycle · after C1 · story: the ¹⁴C tracer that found the cycle (4 clicks, ≈ 36 s)

| Beat | Click plays | Caption | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Lollipop | The lab: the lollipop photo appears whole, halftone portraits of Calvin, Benson and Bassham beside it. Click: a puff of ¹⁴CO₂ enters the green flask; a timer reads 30 s; the algae drop into the alcohol below. | From 1945, carbon-14 was cheap. Calvin, Benson and Bassham fed ¹⁴CO₂ to algae in light, then dropped them into alcohol, which stops all reactions. | Calvin 1961, Nobel lecture |
| 2 Shorter and shorter | The 30 s film appears (dozens of spots). Click: the timer steps 30 s → 5 s → under 1 s and the film beside it changes each time: fewer spots, until one dark spot is left, labelled 3-PGA. | The shorter the feed, the fewer the labelled compounds. After fractions of a second, over 80 % of the ¹⁴C sat in one compound: 3-PGA. | Calvin 1961, Nobel lecture, Figs 5, 6 |
| 3 Spots into a cycle | Camera glides to the 30 s film beside an empty wheel. Click: the spots take their phase colours (3-PGA green, sugar phosphates blue, bisphosphates amber, sucrose rust) and each moves to its place on the wheel; the arcs draw between them. | Naming the spots took ten years. Their order gave the cycle: CO₂ joins RuBP and gives 3-PGA; 3-PGA becomes triose phosphate; most triose phosphate rebuilds RuBP. | Calvin 1961; Bassham et al. 1954 |
| 4 Per 3 CO₂ | The slide's wheel (C1 v1). Click: three orange CO₂ squares enter at the top; six triose phosphates gather at the bottom; five run up the regeneration arc, one leaves. | Per 3 CO₂ fixed, six triose phosphates are made: five rebuild RuBP, one is the gain. **Which enzyme adds the CO₂?** | Sharkey 2019 |

Stagings:
- **A · Zoom on the slide's wheel.** Opens on C1 v1; zooms into the carboxylation arc, cuts to the lab (beats 1–2) and returns along the same zoom to the whole wheel (beats 3–4).
- **B · Newspaper clippings.** Bristol paper with clippings: the Nobel Prize 1961 announcement, the three halftone portraits as avatars leading each beat, the two Nobel films as journal clippings; ends on the wheel.
- **C · The films alone.** No portraits or lab: the 30 s film fills the stage, spots fade out as the timer shortens, then the remaining spots morph into the wheel (C1 v8 style).
- **D · Side by side.** The 30 s film left, the wheel right, a coloured link drawn spot by spot (spot → place on the wheel) on each click.

## cb-rubisco · after C2 · story: slow, so abundant (4 clicks, ≈ 34 s)

| Beat | Click plays | Caption | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 One site | Zoom from the wheel's carboxylation arc into Goodsell's spinach Rubisco; one of the 8 active sites glows. A counter "CO₂ fixed" is on screen from the start. Click: the counter ticks 1, 2, 3 in one second, and keeps that pace. | Rubisco has 8 active sites. Each fixes about 3 CO₂ per second, at 25 °C. | Bar-On & Milo 2019 |
| 2 How much a leaf holds | Camera to a bar of the leaf's nitrogen beside the structure. Click: the green Rubisco share fills to ≈ 20 %; a second bar fills to ≈ 40 % of soluble protein. | So a C₃ leaf holds Rubisco in bulk: about a fifth of its nitrogen, about 40 % of its soluble protein. | Evans & Clarke 2019 |

| 4 Back to the cycle | Zoom out to the wheel; 3-PGA forms; the reduction arc starts to light. | Rubisco makes 3-PGA, an acid, not a sugar. **What turns it into sugar, and what does that cost?** | — |

## cb-energy · after C3 · story: Bassham switches off the light (4 clicks, ≈ 36 s)

| Beat | Click plays | Caption | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 The fuel | Thylakoid membrane strip above the wheel; counters "ATP" and "NADPH" on screen. Click: ATP and NADPH chips flow down into reduction (6 + 6) and regeneration (3 ATP); the counters stop at 9 ATP and 6 NADPH per 3 CO₂. | Per 3 CO₂, reduction takes 6 ATP and 6 NADPH, regeneration 3 ATP: 3 ATP and 2 NADPH per CO₂. | Sharkey 2019, Fig. 3 |
| 2 Lamp off | Camera to Bassham's Chlorella flask under a lamp, with the readout graph already showing the steady 3-PGA and RuBP lines. Click: the lamp goes off, and in the same move 3-PGA jumps and RuBP falls to near zero. | Bassham switched off the light: within a minute 3-PGA piled up and RuBP fell to near zero. | Calvin 1961, Nobel lecture, Fig. 15 |
| 3 Why | Back to the wheel. Click: the ATP and NADPH chips fade; the reduction and regeneration arcs turn grey; the 3-PGA box swells, the RuBP box empties; the carboxylation arc runs once more and stops. | Without ATP and NADPH, 3-PGA is not reduced and RuBP is not rebuilt. Rubisco uses up the RuBP left, then stops. | Calvin 1961 |
| 4 One in six | Light back on; the wheel turns; the one triose phosphate per 3 CO₂ leaves and travels to the chloroplast edge. | **Where does this triose phosphate go, by day and at night?** | — |

Stagings:
- **A · Zoom on the slide's wheel** (C3 v3 look), with the energy chips as the moving parts.
- **B · History-led.** Bassham's halftone portrait (LBL) and the Nobel Fig. 15 as a clipping; a cursor runs along the scan to "light off".
- **C · Real-scan-led.** The original Fig. 15 scan fills the stage; the wheel appears as an inset only in beat 3.
- **D · Light vs dark side by side.** Two wheels, lamp on (left) and lamp off (right), each with its curve below; balanced.

## cb-export · after C4 · story: the early night (Graf et al. 2010) (4 clicks, ≈ 38 s)

| Beat | Click plays | Caption | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Out or in | Zoom from the wheel's exit to the chloroplast envelope. Click: a triose phosphate passes the translocator as a phosphate comes in; in the cytosol, sucrose forms and leaves; inside, a starch granule grows. | Triose phosphate leaves the chloroplast in exchange for phosphate and becomes sucrose, for the whole plant. Part stays and becomes starch. | Sharkey 2024; Smith & Stitt 2007 |
| 2 Day and night | The two Arabidopsis micrographs (end of day, end of night) over a 0–24 h time axis. Click: the starch curve draws through the day and night, ending near zero at dawn. | In Arabidopsis leaves, starch builds up all day and is broken down at night at a near-constant rate: about 95 % is gone by dawn. | Graf et al. 2010; Streb & Zeeman 2012 |

Click: the night band jumps to start at 8 h; the second curve draws, shallower, and reaches zero at the same dawn. | 2010: Graf and colleagues brought night after 8 hours instead of 12.
