## 1. Hook question

| | Candidate | Step-by-step explanation the lecture gives |
|---|---|---|
| **a (recommended)** | **"If Rubisco keeps grabbing O₂ by mistake, why hasn't evolution fixed it in 3 billion years?"** | 1. Rubisco evolved ≈ 3 billion years ago, when the air held much CO₂ and almost no O₂, so its O₂ reaction cost nothing.<br>2. O₂ rose (the Great Oxidation Event, 2.4–2.2 billion years ago) and CO₂ fell, and the O₂ reaction became a real loss.<br>3. Telling CO₂ from O₂ apart makes the enzyme slower: the specificity–speed trade-off.<br>4. Plant Rubisco evolves very slowly. It is tuned to ≈ 210 ppm CO₂, the average of the last 400,000 years, not to today's 427 ppm.<br>5. So plants recycle the product of the O₂ reaction (photorespiration, slide C6), recapture the CO₂ it releases (C11), or pump CO₂ to Rubisco (C₄, CAM). |
| b | "If plants make the oxygen we breathe, why does oxygen slow them down?" | This is already candidate b of the C5–C8 hook box. The story is the same as a, told from the O₂ side. |
| c | "Would plants do better with the CO₂ we add to the air?" | This leads to C14. It belongs there, not here. |

## 2. Key facts (checked; numbers as published)

| # | Fact | Numbers | Source (read depth) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rubisco is older than the O₂ in the air** | Rubisco is ≈ 3 billion years old, possibly 3.5. It evolved when CO₂ was ≥ 100 × today's level and O₂ was ≤ 0.1 % of today's. The Great Oxidation Event was ≈ 2.43 → 2.22 billion years ago (Poulton); Luo gives 2.33. The small subunit raised specificity before O₂ appeared in the air (Schulz 2022). | Bouvier, Emms & Kelly 2024 PNAS 121:e2321050121, CC BY (full text); Iñiguez et al. 2020 Plant J 101:897 (abstract); Poulton et al. 2021 Nature 592:232 (abstract); Luo et al. 2016 Sci Adv 2:e1600134 (abstract); Schulz et al. 2022 Science 378:155 (abstract) |
| 2 | **Specificity–speed trade-off** | Data: 304 organisms. CO₂/O₂ specificity (S꜀/ₒ): bacterial form II 7–15; cyanobacteria median 48; C₄ plants median 80; **C₃ plants median 98** (N = 162); **red algae median 177, maximum 238** (*Galdieria partita*). k꜀ₐₜ: > 95 % of values are 1–10 s⁻¹; C₃ plants 2.5–3.7 s⁻¹ per site. The trade-off is weaker with the full data set: R ≈ −0.9 in older data vs −0.56 now. | Flamholz et al. 2019 Biochemistry 58:3365 (full text); Tcherkez, Farquhar & Andrews 2006 PNAS 103:7246, Table 1 (full text) |
| 2b | **Why the trade-off exists** | A transition state that tells CO₂ from O₂ apart holds the carboxylated intermediate tightly, so its cleavage, the next step, slows. In 28 Rubiscos the kinetic constants lie near 1 line; 1 principal component holds ≈ 91 % of the variance. | Tcherkez 2006 (full text); Savir et al. 2010 PNAS 107:3475 (full text) |
| 3 | **Tuned to a past CO₂** | The average specificity of crop Rubisco is optimal for **210 ppm**, "close to the average of the last 400,000 y", and too high for 370 ppm. A Rubisco with the lower specificity that suits today would add only **+3 %** canopy carbon gain. **> 25 %** needs foreign Rubiscos that beat the trade-off (higher specificity or higher k꜀ₐₜ). Ice-core CO₂ over 800,000 years: **172–300 ppm**. Pre-industrial (1750): 278 ppm. Mauna Loa annual mean 2025: **427.35 ppm**. | Zhu, Portis & Long 2004 PCE 27:155 (abstract via USDA ARS; full text not read); Lüthi et al. 2008 Nature 453:379 (abstract); IPCC AR6 (secondary); NOAA GML co2_annmean_mlo.txt |
| 4 | **Rubisco evolves slowly** | rbcL is among the 1 % slowest-evolving genes: 1 nucleotide change per 0.9 million years, **1 amino-acid change per 7.2 million years**. Specificity improves **+0.05 % per million years**, k꜀ₐₜ +0.2 % per million years. Last common ancestor of flowering plants vs today: S꜀/ₒ 81.1 → 87.1, k꜀ₐₜ 2.6 → 3.4 s⁻¹. CO₂ rose 278 → 427 ppm (+54 %) in ≈ 275 years. "Faster than evolution can follow" is **our inference** from these rates; no paper read states it. | Bouvier 2024 (full text; SI not read) |
| 5 | **How plants live with it** | **Refixation:** in rice and wheat, chloroplasts cover the wall facing the air spaces (> 95 % of the rice cell periphery). Inside the cell, 24–38 % of the photorespired and respired CO₂ is refixed. Net uptake: +8–11 % at ambient CO₂, +17–33 % at 200 ppm, which may explain why C₃ plants persisted through the low CO₂ of the Pleistocene. Deck slide C11 uses Busch Table 1 (photorespired CO₂ only): 29 % (rice), 26 % (wheat). **C₂, C₄ (> 60 origins), CAM** (slides C11–C13). **Engineered bypass** in field tobacco: biomass **+19 to +37 %** across replicated field trials. Pathway 3 with *PLGG1* RNAi: **+20 % (2016), +24 % (2017)**. Quantum yield +20 %. Photorespiration can cut C₃ yields by 20–50 %. | Busch, Sage, Cousins & Sage 2013 PCE 36:200 (abstract; the deck builder read the full text); Sage & Sage 2009 PCP 50:756 (abstract); South, Cavanagh, Liu & Ort 2019 Science 363:eaat9077, corrected version, PMC7745124, CC BY (full text) |

**Corrections to the brief:**
- **South 2019:** "≈ 40 % biomass" is the **pre-correction** abstract. The erratum (Science 365:eaay8818, Aug 2019) corrected it to "19 to 37 %". The ≈ 40 % figure in the main text refers to starch.
- **Bouvier 2024** is in **PNAS**, not Science.
- **Zhu 2004:** the optimum is 210 ppm, the 400,000-year mean, not the glacial minimum. A "faster, less specific" Rubisco would gain only ≈ 3 %. The large gains (> 25 %) need foreign Rubiscos that lie above the trade-off line, e.g. red-algal ones.
- **Busch 2013:** the abstract gives 24–38 % (photorespired and respired CO₂ together) and +8–11 %. The 26–29 % and +9–11 % on C11 come from its Table 1 (photorespired CO₂ only). Both are right; they measure different things.

**Oxygenation in different eras (our calculation, not a published table):**
- Method: FvCB, vₒ/v꜀ = 2Γ*/C꜀, with the deck's own values: Γ* = 40 ppm at 25 °C (as in rf8), and C꜀ ≈ 0.48 × Cₐ (the deck's C10 values, C꜀ 200 at Cₐ 420).
- Results:
  - Glacial air, 180 ppm: C꜀ ≈ 86, vₒ/v꜀ ≈ 0.93, i.e. ≈ 1 oxygenation per 1 carboxylation.
  - Pre-industrial air, 278 ppm: C꜀ ≈ 133, vₒ/v꜀ ≈ 0.60, i.e. 3 per 5.
  - Today, 427 ppm: C꜀ ≈ 205, vₒ/v꜀ ≈ 0.39, i.e. ≈ 2 per 5.
- Caveat: constant C꜀/Cₐ is a simplification. These are illustrative numbers for a figure, flagged as calculated.

## 6. Doubts

- Zhu 2004 was read from the abstract only. The 210 ppm optimum and the +3 % / > 25 % are from the abstract. The *Griffithsia* +27 % appears only in a secondary report: do not use it.
- The era-by-era vₒ/v꜀ values are our own FvCB calculation with a constant C꜀/Cₐ. A figure must label them "calculated".
- No paper read says outright that "CO₂ rises faster than Rubisco can evolve"; present it as an inference from Bouvier's rates.
- Flamholz shows the trade-off is weaker than once thought (R −0.56). "Tend to be slower" is the honest wording, not "must be slower".
