# C10 → C11 → C11a: why CO₂ is hard to get, and the first answers (lecturer, 29 Oct 2026)

## Take-home message per slide (everything on screen serves it)
- **C10, CO₂ uptake.**
  - CO₂ moves into the leaf only by diffusion, down a concentration gradient: RuBisCO uses CO₂ up in the chloroplast, so C꜀ is low and the air is high.
  - On the way the CO₂ crosses resistances in series: the boundary layer, the stoma, the air spaces, then it must dissolve in water at the cell wall and cross the wall, membrane and cytosol to the chloroplast.
  - Each resistance lowers the CO₂ at RuBisCO; Fick's law: CO₂ uptake = conductance × CO₂ drop.
  - Heat and drought make it harder: stomata close. In warmer water CO₂ dissolves less relative to O₂.
  - So in C₃ leaves, CO₂ at RuBisCO is low, and oxygenation (photorespiration) is high.
- **C11, C₃ leaves: refixation.** The first answer is to reuse the CO₂ that photorespiration releases. C₃ leaves place their chloroplasts between the mitochondria and the air spaces, so photorespired CO₂ passes RuBisCO on its way out and part of it is refixed.
- **C11a, C₂ photosynthesis.** C₂ plants go further. Glycine moves into the bundle-sheath cells, the only place where it is decarboxylated, in mitochondria that are surrounded by chloroplasts. The released CO₂ builds up next to RuBisCO and is refixed.
- The chunk then continues with C12 (C₄, a pump) and C13 (CAM, timing). Those are not part of this brief.

### lf10, ≈ 35 s
1. **The gradient.**
   - CO₂-kid in the outside air (Cₐ ≈ 430 ppm); RuBisCO in a chloroplast fixing CO₂ (a little "used" tick).
   - A gradient bar beside the section: high outside, low at RuBisCO.
   - Caption: "RuBisCO uses up CO₂ in the chloroplast, so CO₂ diffuses in from the air, down its gradient."
2. **Boundary layer.** The kid crosses the still-air band and slows with a strained face. Caption: "First the boundary layer: still air at the leaf surface."
3. **Stoma.**
   - The kid squeezes through the pore (inset: the pore); H₂O molecules leave past it, several per CO₂.
   - Value: Cᵢ ≈ 300 ppm.
   - Caption: "Then the stoma; water vapour leaves through the same pore."
4. **Air spaces and the wet wall.**
   - The kid drifts through the air spaces to a cell. At the wall it dissolves (inset: water shell) and slows strongly.
   - Caption: "At the cell wall CO₂ must dissolve in water; in water it diffuses ≈ 10,000 times more slowly than in air."
5. **To RuBisCO.**
   - The dissolved kid crosses the membrane, cytosol and chloroplast envelope to RuBisCO and is fixed.
   - Value: C꜀ ≈ 200 ppm.
   - The staircase 430 → 300 → 200 ppm with the conductances (gₛ, gₘ) and *A = g × ΔC*.
6. **Hot, dry day.**
   - The stomata half-close and the kid gets stuck; the staircase drops lower and RuBisCO gets more O₂.
   - Caption: "In heat and drought the stomata close; less CO₂ reaches RuBisCO, and oxygenation rises. <b>How have plants coped?</b>"

### lf11, ≈ 30 s
1. **Photorespiration releases CO₂.** Oxygenation in a chloroplast → glycine → mitochondrion → CO₂ 1–4 released, numbered.
2. **Position matters.**
   - Two cells side by side. Left (hypothetical): mitochondria at the wall, so all 4 CO₂ escape to the air space. Right (C₃ leaf, e.g. rice and wheat): chloroplasts cover the wall facing the air space, and the mitochondria sit behind them.
   - Caption: "C₃ leaves place chloroplasts between the mitochondria and the air spaces."
3. **Refixation.**
   - In the right cell, CO₂ 1 is caught by RuBisCO on its way out; CO₂ 2–4 reach the air space, where neighbouring cells catch part of them.
   - Counter: refixed inside the cell 26–29 %, net CO₂ uptake +9–11 % (Busch et al. 2013).
4. **Heat.** More oxygenation releases more CO₂ than the chloroplasts catch. Caption: "<b>Could a plant release this CO₂ where RuBisCO is concentrated?</b>"

### lf11a, ≈ 25 s
1. **Glycine moves.** Glycine 1–2 travel from the mesophyll into a bundle-sheath cell.
2. **Decarboxylation next to RuBisCO.**
   - Bundle-sheath mitochondria along the inner wall, surrounded by chloroplasts; glycine decarboxylase releases CO₂ 1, which builds up and is refixed by bundle-sheath RuBisCO; serine returns.
   - Caption: "C₂ plants release photorespired CO₂ only in the bundle sheath, where chloroplasts surround the mitochondria."
3. **Build-up.** A CO₂ level bar for the bundle sheath: C₂ ≈ 3 × C₃ (leaf mean, *Flaveria*; Keerberg et al. 2014). Caption ends: "<b>What if the leaf pumped CO₂ to RuBisCO directly?</b>" (C₄ next).

## Facts to verify before building (fact table in the report)
- Chloroplast cover of the cell periphery and the position of mitochondria and stromules in rice and wheat: Sage & Sage 2009, Plant Cell Physiol; Busch et al. 2013, PCE. Read in full: the lecturer is on the ETH VPN; plain curl or WebFetch; no bot-check circumvention.
- C₂ bundle-sheath mitochondria positions: Sage, Khoshravesh & Sage 2014, JXB; Rawsthorne 1988.
- ppm values: Busch et al. 2024.
- Diffusion in water vs air, ≈ 10⁴: diffusion coefficients of CO₂, 1.6 × 10⁻⁵ m² s⁻¹ in air vs 1.9 × 10⁻⁹ in water; give the source.
- **Warming:** only the effects that are true. CO₂ solubility falls with temperature, and faster than O₂ solubility (Sander 2023). Do not claim that mesophyll conductance falls with warming (it often rises up to ≈ 35 °C; von Caemmerer & Evans 2015).
