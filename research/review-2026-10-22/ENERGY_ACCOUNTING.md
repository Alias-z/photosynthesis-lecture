# Electron routing and carbon demand

**Instructor context — NOT YET APPROVED.** These are explicit bookkeeping examples for reviewing the lecture, not fixed measured yields for every leaf.

## Keep four quantities separate

An electron route determines where electrons go and how proton movement can accompany them. ATP synthase converts proton motive force into ATP. Metabolic pathways determine ATP/reductant consumption. Measured ATP or NADPH concentrations reflect both supply and consumption; they are not production fluxes.

The numerical table assumes a complete cytochrome b₆f Q cycle, tight coupling and the structural plant chloroplast ratio **14 H⁺ / 3 ATP**. Fractional yields are molar/ensemble averages. These assumptions must remain visible whenever a numerical yield is used.

## A common route basis

| Route | Counting basis | Minimum productive photons | H⁺ supplied to lumen or translocated | Net NADPH | Ideal ATP supported | Ideal ATP per productive photon |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Linear: water → NADP⁺ | 4 e⁻ transferred | 8: 4 PSII + 4 PSI | 12 | 2 | 18/7 ≈ 2.57 | 9/28 ≈ 0.321 |
| Cyclic, non-pumping Fd→PQ return | 4 completed electron circuits | 4 PSI | 8 | 0 | 12/7 ≈ 1.71 | 3/7 ≈ 0.429 |
| Cyclic via proton-pumping NDH | 4 completed electron circuits | 4 PSI | Approximately 16 | 0 | 24/7 ≈ 3.43 | 6/7 ≈ 0.857 |
| Ideal complete water→PSII→PSI→O₂ route | 4 e⁻ ultimately used to reduce O₂ | 8: 4 PSII + 4 PSI | 12 under the same upstream coupling account | 0 retained | 18/7 ≈ 2.57, conditionally | 9/28 ≈ 0.321, conditionally |

The last row is an **ideal full-chain bookkeeping limit**, not a universal measured water–water yield. The location of O₂ reduction, ROS chemistry, detoxification, other sinks and proton leakage must be included for a real pathway. Classical Mehler-peroxidase chemistry differs from FLV-mediated O₂ reduction. Native angiosperms lack photosynthetic FLVs. A four-electron gross production of one O₂ at PSII balanced by four-electron uptake of one O₂ gives zero net O₂ for the complete water–water account.

The linear row also gives **0.25 NADPH per productive photon** and **0.125 O₂ per productive photon**. Those are ideal stoichiometric yields. They are not PAM ΦPSII, incident-light quantum yield, or an energy efficiency percentage.

For linear flow, the 12 H⁺ account is **4 from water oxidation + 8 from net PQ/b₆f Q-cycle coupling**. Do not add PQ uptake again. NADPH formation consumes stromal protons but is not another independent proton pump. The approximately two extra pumped H⁺ per electron in NDH underlies the different cyclic result.

Sources: [Hahn et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat4318), [Strand et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M116.770792), [Kramer and Evans 2011](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.110.166652), [Asada 1999](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.arplant.50.1.601).

## What should be compared in class

One linear-flow calculation is enough. Compare the other routes mainly by electron source/sink, net NADPH and proton coupling. If an additional worked example is wanted: four linear electrons plus one non-pumping cyclic circuit produce 14 H⁺ and two NADPH, ideally supporting three ATP with nine productive photons. This does **not** establish a compulsory cyclic fraction.

| State | Relevant consequence | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| NADP⁺ is regenerated by active metabolism | Linear electrons can reach a useful sink | No need for a fixed hierarchy of pathways |
| PSI acceptors are strongly reduced | Electron delivery, cycling and terminal outlets can change | Cyclic flow recirculates; an O₂ outlet removes net reducing equivalents |
| ATP/reductant demand differs among pathways or cell types | The useful proton/reductant supply ratio changes | C₃ versus C₄ and mesophyll versus bundle sheath must be distinguished |
| Lumen acidifies | ATP synthesis and protective regulation interact | pmf is both energetic and regulatory |
| ATP concentration is unchanged | Production and consumption may have changed together | Pool stability does not establish unchanged flux |

Recent evidence: [Degen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-026-02374-w), [Nikkanen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbabio.2026.149596), [Nakamura et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiag146). The first measures an Arabidopsis ATP pool; the second proposes regulatory functions; the third tests a C₄ system. They should not be flattened into one universal verdict about cyclic transport.

## Carbon demand and its boundary

For the canonical fully recycling C₃ model:

- ATP demand = **3 Vc + 3.5 Vo**
- Reductant demand = **2 Vc + 2 Vo NADPH equivalents**
- Carbon gain before other respiration = **Vc − 0.5 Vo**

Vc and Vo are carboxylation and oxygenation fluxes in the same units. The oxygenation term includes its associated recovery and cycle-restoration account. NADPH equivalents aggregate reducing equivalents; they do not mean every compartment directly uses NADPH.

With Vo = 0, the CBB demand is 3 ATP and 2 NADPH per CO₂. If Vo/Vc = 0.3, the canonical demands per net carbon gain before Rᴸ become (3 + 3.5 × 0.3)/(1 − 0.5 × 0.3) ≈ **4.76 ATP**, and (2 + 2 × 0.3)/(1 − 0.5 × 0.3) ≈ **3.06 NADPH equivalents**. This is a worked model example, not a typical-leaf constant.

Carbon leaving photorespiration for glycine, serine or one-carbon metabolism changes the balance. In the standard PPDK-based C₄ account, PEP regeneration adds two ATP equivalents per concentrated CO₂; leakage and cell-specific metabolite exchange alter effective demand.

Sources: [Walker et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.14949), [Gashu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-025-02091-w), [Yin et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.14070).

## FvCB bookkeeping for the instructor

A useful basic formulation applies limitation to carboxylation capacity:

Wc = Vcmax Cc / [Cc + Kc(1 + O/Ko)]

Wj = J Cc / [4(Cc + 2Γ*)]

Vc = min(Wc, Wj)

A = Vc(1 − Γ*/Cc) − Rᴸ

Here Cc and O are substrate concentrations or partial pressures in consistent units, and Γ* is defined consistently. Kc and Ko describe Rubisco substrate kinetics. This formulation assumes the canonical recycling account and the usual reductant-based regeneration limitation. The last expression is written for nonzero Cc; boundary behaviour is handled through its algebraic limit, not numerical division by zero.

For the ordinary positive-assimilation illustration, one can show the corresponding Ac and Aj branches and the limiting rate. Do not assume every min(Ac, Aj, Ap) implementation is equivalent under negative assimilation or TPU extensions. The 2025 analysis specifically cautions against treating different variants as interchangeable.

J is the electron supply represented by the carbon-regeneration account; it is not automatically fluorescence-derived total PSII ETR when alternative sinks operate. Cc is not Ci unless the diffusion assumption is explicitly made.

Sources: [Farquhar et al. 1980](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00386231), [Yin et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.14070), [von Caemmerer et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00425-025-04834-7), [Lochocki and McGrath 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/insilicoplants/diaf014).
