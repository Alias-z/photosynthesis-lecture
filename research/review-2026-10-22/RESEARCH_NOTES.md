The proposal contains 15 light-reaction slots and 12 carbon-reaction slots. Each lecture has 43 minutes of content plus 2 minutes for questions. The first six light slots retain the agreed 14-minute opening. The remaining light sequence gives water oxidation and O₂ a distinct place before electron/proton accounting, and places fluorescence and photoprotection after lumen acidification.

Research was checked on 22–23 October 2026 using the project's existing research notes, the three previous lecture decks, publisher pages, indexed abstracts and selected available full-text sections. The source register distinguishes those evidence levels. This is a curated update, not an exhaustive systematic review or a claim that every complete paper was read.

Selection favours established subject journals, society journals and major reviews, with primary papers supporting new claims. No predatory or questionable venue is selected. MDPI/Frontiers search hits and an unfamiliar SCILTP C₂ review were not used. A recent low-CO₂ acclimation preprint was excluded from the teaching basis: it is unreviewed and outside this lecture's scope. Journal reputation alone does not settle whether a particular interpretation is correct.

Environmental controls, sun/shade acclimation, engineering applications and the discarded photoreceptor discussion remain outside the teaching plan. Brief biochemical regulation and conditions that alter electron routing are necessary to explain the mechanism, not new environmental-control sections. CAM retains a removable two-minute slot.

## What the recent literature changes

- **Cyclic flow:** [Degen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-026-02374-w) challenges an obligatory ATP top-up story in Arabidopsis; it measured MgATP²⁻ concentration, not synthesis flux. [Nikkanen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbabio.2026.149596) proposes an ATP-synthase regulatory role for PGR5. [Nakamura et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiag146) supplies a C₄ comparison. Keep proton coupling, protection and metabolic context together; do not declare cyclic ATP synthesis obsolete.
- **O₂-directed routes:** [Rizzetto et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/ppl.70453) tests moss FLV expressed in tobacco. It illustrates route competition, not native FLV operation in flowering plants. Classical Mehler chemistry and FLV-mediated reduction are different mechanisms.
- **Water oxidation:** [Murray 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/tpj.70925) reviews current structural progress. The four-electron water/O₂ account is firm; some transient atomistic details remain unresolved.
- **NPQ:** [van Amerongen and Croce 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/plcell/koaf240) explicitly presents mechanistic uncertainties. [Nicol et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74992-z) strengthens the LHCII connection in Arabidopsis. Teach the lumen → antenna → dissipation chain without promoting one universal quenching site or percentage.
- **Photorespiration:** [Walker et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.14949) and [Gashu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-025-02091-w) show why a closed salvage loop is a useful first account but not the complete metabolic network. The latter experiment used Arabidopsis; its quantitative fraction is not universal.
- **C₂/C₄ evolution:** [Leung et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.15069) and [Stata et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.70426), including the Sage group, provide current lineage evidence. [Dickinson et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44319-025-00461-1) provides a particularly clear regulatory-reuse story for C₂ in Brassicaceae. Gene names are reserve material.
- **Rubisco:** [Bouvier et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2321050121) counters the claim that plant Rubisco is completely evolutionarily frozen. The [2025 mutational landscape](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08455-0) uses bacterial Form II Rubisco in engineered E. coli; it is not plant-performance evidence.
- **FvCB:** the requested [Yin et al. 2021 review](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.14070) marks **40 years**. The [authors' 2025 retrospective](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00425-025-04834-7) explains the original logic, while [Lochocki and McGrath 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/insilicoplants/diaf014) shows that model variants can differ. These refine the context; they do not require a parameter-fitting lecture.

## Animation selection

Animations are explanatory overlays opened by a small button. The static slide must retain the mechanism and takeaway when exported to PDF. No new animation or graphic has been generated in this task.

| Priority | Sequence | Maximum playback | Why motion helps |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Existing | Sky and sunset | 60–90 s | Reuse the original, unchanged |
| High | Charge separation → water electrons → two photosystems → protons → ATP | About 90 s across L07–L10 | Distinguishes excitation, electron movement and proton movement within one consistent membrane |
| High | Lumen acidification → antenna dissipation/xanthophyll cycle | 30–45 s | Explains causal order instead of an isolated pigment list |
| High | Carbon reduction and RuBP regeneration | 45–60 s | Makes the one-out/five-retained carbon balance visible |
| High | Photorespiration → C₂ → C₄ on the same anatomy | 75–100 s across C06/C08/C09 | Shows why the location and carrier of carbon matter |
| Secondary | Excitation transfer in the already agreed LHC brief | 20–30 s | Helpful, but not needed before the priority sequences |
| Secondary | Electron-route toggles | 20 s | Reuse the membrane; avoid competing moving streams |

The individual segment limits in the draft cards are caps, not a request to play every segment at its maximum. A whole shared membrane walkthrough should take approximately 90 seconds; remove repeated openings and pauses. Static carbon/isotope diagrams, pigment spectra, the solar spectrum, fluorescence formulae and FvCB curves do not need separate animations.
