# Independent review: L12–L15 (excitation fates, photoprotection, fluorescence, light-response curve)

Reviewer: Claude (independent check, 23 Oct 2026). Source checked: `research/review-2026-10-22/CONTEXT_REVIEW.md` §L12–L15 and the matching `drafts.json` entries. No project file other than this report was changed.

**Evidence levels.** A = relevant full-text sections read (Europe PMC/publisher HTML). B = abstract or publisher landing page read. C = metadata only (Crossref record resolves; authors, year, journal, pages match). S = secondary source only. Retraction/correction check: Crossref `updated-by` and Europe PMC flags for every cited DOI.

**Bottom line.** All 15 cited works exist, and authors, year and journal match. None is retracted. Johnson 2025 carries a minor Author Correction: a unit typo, "~0.25 M" corrected to "~0.25 mM" ([10.1038/s41580-025-00877-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41580-025-00877-6)). The physiology is mostly sound. The problems are framing and scope:
- **Photodamage (L13).** It is framed as something that happens only when protection fails. That is outdated.
- **Blackman's limiting factors (L15).** Presented as a sharp switch from one limit to the next, which overstates what real leaves show.
- **Citation scope.** Three citations point at papers that do not test the sentence they support: Nikkanen 2026 in L13, Degen/Nakamura 2026 in L14, Sharkey 2024 in L15.
- **Gaps.** The drafts give no numbers. The slow NPQ components and the crop-engineering controversy are missing. So is any remote-sensing (SIF) link, although that was part of the brief.

## Currency notes (2023–2026)

**NPQ mechanism and site**
- **Agreed so far:** ΔpH → PsbS protonation (+ Zx) → quenching in the PSII antenna.
  - Most quenching sits in LHCII trimers ([Nicol 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74992-z), A).
  - The minor antennae are not the primary site. CP29 mutants reach wild-type NPQ, and an in vivo study excluded CP26 ([vAC 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/plcell/koaf240), A).
- **Still open:**
  - The quencher's photophysics. Chl→carotenoid energy transfer: [Ruban 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06262); Chl→Zx transfer correlating with qE, [Lee 2024 PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2411620121). Carotenoid charge transfer: [Holt 2005](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1105833). Chl–Chl charge-transfer states.
  - Where PsbS binds, and its stoichiometry (~1 PsbS per 2–4 PSII).
  - Whether Zx speeds NPQ induction. [Ramakers 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/ppl.70420) says no (single-lab analysis).
  - Lecture-level message: teach the causal chain firmly and the molecular quencher as open. The draft already does this; keep it.
- **Components.** qE (seconds to minutes), qZ (Zx-dependent, slower), qT (state transitions), qI (hours; photoinhibition) and qH (sustained). They are defined operationally by kinetics and overlap ([vAC 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/plcell/koaf240); [Ramakers 2025 New Phytol](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.20271)).
- **qH.**
  - Requires the lipocalin LCNP ([Malnoë 2018](https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.17.00536)); SOQ1 suppresses it; ROQH1 relaxes it ([Amstutz 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-020-0591-9)).
  - It can occur in LHCII trimers ([Bru 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbc.2022.102519)). Review: [Malnoë 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envexpbot.2018.05.005).
  - Preprints only: SOQ1 as a methionine sulfoxide reductase acting on LCNP ([Hao 2024, bioRxiv](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.11.01.621559)); minor antennae as secondary qH sites ([Bru 2025, bioRxiv](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.02.11.637767)).
  - vAC 2025 regards qH as "active only at low temperatures", so its physiological breadth is disputed.
  - **Instructor note only.**
- **Faster NPQ relaxation in crops (mixed, species-dependent; not settled):**
  - Tobacco: VPZ gave ~15% more biomass in fluctuating light ([Kromdijk 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aai8878)).
  - Soybean: up to 33% more seed yield in five events ([De Souza 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adc9831)). This was disputed ([Sinclair 2023 comment](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ade8506)) and defended ([De Souza 2023 response](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adf2189)). [Croce 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/plcell/koae132) summarises the soybean gain as ~20%.
  - Null or negative results:
    - Arabidopsis: [Garcia-Molina & Leister 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-019-0572-z).
    - Potato: [Lehretz 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/jipb.13320).
    - **Rice, 2026:** impaired growth, including in the field ([Zheng 2026 PCE](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.70245)).
    - **Soybean follow-up, 2026:** VPZ-34A showed better light-use efficiency but no seed or biomass gain at ambient or elevated CO₂ in the year measured ([Singh 2026 PCE](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.70608)).
  - **C₄ species already relax NPQ faster than C₃** relatives, via a larger qE share ([Arce Cubas 2026 PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2621033123)).
  - vAC 2025's "yield increases up to 30%" is the optimistic end of this range.

**Fluorescence as a modern measurement / SIF**
- **Satellite SIF:**
  - GOSAT: [Frankenberg 2011](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011GL048738); [Joiner 2011](https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-8-637-2011).
  - OCO-2: a linear SIF–GPP relation at flux sites ([Sun 2017 Science](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aam5747)).
  - **ESA FLEX, the first dedicated fluorescence mission, launched on 15 September 2026** with Sentinel-3C on Vega-C. No commissioning or first-light items were posted on the [ESA FLEX page](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/FutureEO/FLEX) at the time of checking. This is a timely hook for students.
- **Caveats:**
  - At canopy scale, SIF ≈ APAR × Φ_F × escape fraction. The SIF–GPP correlation is driven mostly by APAR and canopy structure, with a weak physiological term ([Dechant 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2020.111733)).
  - Physiology shows up under stress: a heatwave broke linearity via NPQ saturation ([Martini 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.17920)). A 2025 letter argues the opposite ([Antala 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.20312), C).
  - Reviews: [Porcar-Castell 2021 Nat Plants](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-021-00980-4); [Sun 2023 GCB](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.16634).
- **Teaching point.** SIF is passive (no saturating pulse), so it cannot give Fv/Fm or ΦPSII directly. That makes it the perfect contrast with PAM on L14.

## Gaps (essential for a BSc, missing or too thin)

1. **Photoinhibition as a damage–repair balance and the D1 repair cycle.**

## Story candidates ("then → now")

1. **Kautsky 1931: two discoveries in one season → FLEX in orbit 2026.** *(top)*
   - Then: [Kautsky & Hirsch 1931](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01516164) reported the fluorescence induction effect. A month later, [Kautsky & de Bruijn 1931](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01516190) reported active, diffusible oxygen, now called singlet oxygen. Kautsky wrongly believed this oxygen mechanism drove photosynthesis ([Schreiber & Lichtenthaler 2025](https://doi.org/10.32615/ps.2025.007)).
   - Then the controlled probe: [PAM, Schreiber 1986](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00024185).
   - Now: [OCO-2 SIF](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aam5747), and [FLEX launched 15 Sep 2026](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/FutureEO/FLEX).
   - Why it works: one man's two observations become the two halves of L12–L14, fluorescence as the window and ¹O₂ as the danger.
2. **The xanthophyll cycle → engineered crops, and the field pushes back.** *(top)*
   - Then: Sapozhnikov 1957 (his own account: [Sapozhnikov 1973](https://doi.org/10.1351/pac197335010047)) and [Yamamoto 1962](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-9861(62)90060-7); [Demmig 1987](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.84.2.218) linked Zx to photoprotection; the *npq1*/*npq4* mutants ([Niyogi 1998](https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.10.7.1121); [Li 2000](https://doi.org/10.1038/35000131)).
   - Turning point: [Kromdijk 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aai8878) and [De Souza 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adc9831).
   - Now (2026): a rice failure ([Zheng 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.70245)), a null soybean year ([Singh 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.70608)), and C₄ crops already fast ([Arce Cubas 2026](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2621033123)).
   - Why it works: it shows a live, unresolved controversy.
3. **Blackman 1905 (and Gabrielle Matthaei's data) → smooth co-limitation and FvCB.**
   - Then: [Blackman 1905](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.aob.a089000); [Matthaei 1905 Phil Trans B](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1905.0005); [Blackman & Matthaei 1905](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1905.0037).
   - Now: [Ögren & Evans 1993](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00195075) (Θ < 1) and FvCB's min() ([Farquhar 1980](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00386231); [Yin 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.14070)). It also credits an under-recognised woman experimentalist.
4. **Damage even in dim light.**
   - Then: [Tyystjärvi & Aro 1996](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.93.5.2213) blocked repair with lincomycin and saw photoinhibition at only 6.5 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹.
   - Now: the FtsH/D1 repair cycle ([Komenda 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/plcell/koae082)).
5. **One number from many plants.**
   - Then: [Björkman & Demmig 1987](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00402983) found φ = 0.106 and Fv/Fm = 0.832 across 37 C₃ species. [Ehleringer & Björkman 1977](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.59.1.86) showed that 2% O₂ raises C₃ but not C₄ quantum yield.
   - Now: this sets up photorespiration and C₄ in lecture 2.
