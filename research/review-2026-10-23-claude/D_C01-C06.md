# Review D: C01–C06 and the carbon-demand formulas

Independent check, 23 October 2026. Scope: `CONTEXT_REVIEW.md` sections C01–C06 (identical to `drafts.json`, including the `optional` reserve notes), plus "Carbon demand and its boundary" in `ENERGY_ACCOUNTING.md`. I made no changes to project files except writing this report.

**Method.** I checked every cited DOI against Crossref, including its `updated-by` field for corrections and retractions. I read abstracts through Europe PMC or PubMed. Where an open full text existed I searched it (Europe PMC full-text XML for Sharkey 2024, von Caemmerer et al. 2025, Kramer & Evans 2011, South et al. 2019 author manuscript, Prywes et al. 2025 and Meacham-Hensold et al. 2026). I recomputed every stoichiometry myself.

**Evidence levels.** **A** = I read the relevant full-text passage or figure. **B** = abstract or publisher metadata. **C** = an authors' retrospective, a secondary account or standard textbook knowledge. **D** = my own calculation.

**Bottom line.** I checked 74 claims. None is WRONG or OUTDATED. Three are UNSUPPORTED because the cited paper does not cover the claim (the claims themselves are correct). One citation is UNVERIFIED. Two statements are imprecise in wording. The stoichiometry and the FvCB demand formulas are all correct. The main weaknesses are gaps: where fixed carbon goes (the promised link to wheat-grain starch is never delivered), light activation of the cycle, and a weak treatment of "dark reactions".

## Currency notes (2023–2026)

1. **Photorespiration as a metabolic hub, with the magnitude unsettled.** Fu et al. 2023 ([10.1038/s41477-022-01294-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-022-01294-9)) estimate about 23–41 % of photorespiratory carbon exits as serine. Gashu et al. 2025 put about 5.8 % of assimilated carbon into C1 metabolism. Sharkey 2024 questions the high serine figures. Busch et al. 2018 ([10.1038/s41477-017-0065-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-017-0065-x)) link this export to nitrogen assimilation and higher CO₂ uptake. The drafts handle this with appropriate scope. Adding Fu 2023 and Sharkey's caveat would show students the live disagreement.
2. **The energy-balance story is shifting.** Walker et al. 2024 show open-loop photorespiration can lower the ATP:NADPH demand below linear-flow supply. Degen et al. 2026 find ATP pools unchanged without PGR5- or NDH-dependent cyclic flow. Together they weaken the textbook "cyclic flow must top up ATP" argument. The C04 wording ("worth asking", not "proves") is right; keep it.
3. **Rubisco: constrained but engineerable.** Davidi et al. 2020 found a Form II enzyme with kcat 22 s⁻¹, about six times the median plant enzyme ([10.15252/embj.2019104081](https://doi.org/10.15252/embj.2019104081)); this updates Flamholz's "none above 15 s⁻¹". Zhou et al. 2023 grafted red-algal loop-6 residues into *R. sphaeroides* Rubisco, raising kcat by 60 % and doubling tobacco growth relative to the wild-type *R. sphaeroides* Rubisco line ([10.1038/s41477-023-01436-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-023-01436-7)). McDonald et al. 2025 directed evolution in *E. coli* to give a Gallionellaceae Rubisco oxygen resistance ([10.1073/pnas.2505083122](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2505083122)). Newest: McDonald et al., *Science Advances*, 9 Oct 2026, used ML-assisted directed evolution of tobacco RbcL; T391I gave +29 % kcat and +43 % aerobic carboxylation efficiency ([10.1126/sciadv.aeh3246](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeh3246)). I read only the bioRxiv abstract, and no whole-plant data were reported. All of this was enabled by assembling plant Rubisco in *E. coli* (Aigner et al. 2017, [10.1126/science.aap9221](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aap9221)). A shorter-term route is simply more Rubisco, about +10 % productivity in field trials (Salesse-Smith et al. 2025 review, [10.1111/nph.20298](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.20298)).
4. **Rubisco activase and heat.** Across species and globally, the fall in assimilation above the temperature optimum is explained by Rubisco deactivation plus declining electron transport (Scafaro et al. 2023, [10.1038/s41467-023-38496-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-38496-4)). Activase isoforms differ in heat sensitivity (Amaral et al. 2024, [10.1111/nph.19369](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.19369)).
5. **Thioredoxin regulation now includes a dedicated "off" switch.** The 2-Cys Prx/TrxL2 oxidation pathway (Yoshida et al. 2018; review by Yoshida & Hisabori 2023, [10.1093/pcp/pcad049](https://doi.org/10.1093/pcp/pcad049)) and the solved GAPDH/CP12/PRK structures (Gurrieri et al. 2021) show regulation is active in both directions.
6. **Synthetic photorespiratory bypasses: real gains, small trials.**
   - Tobacco (South et al. 2019, [10.1126/science.aat9077](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat9077); full text read): the headline is ">40 %" for some lines, but the structured summary gives AP3 + PLGG1-RNAi biomass gains of 20 % (2016, P = 0.04) and 24 % (2017, P = 0.018). Quote the latter.
   - Tobacco under +5 °C warming: +26 % versus +11 % at ambient (Cavanagh et al. 2022, [10.1111/pbi.13750](https://doi.org/10.1111/pbi.13750)).
   - Potato tubers after two natural heatwaves (Meacham-Hensold et al. 2024, [10.1111/gcb.17595](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.17595)).
   - The 2026 review (Meacham-Hensold, Jeong, Cavanagh & Ort, New Phytol 249:675–690, [10.1111/nph.70724](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.70724)) lists failures and limits: the rice GOC bypass raised biomass but not seed yield, with lower seed set; the GCGT bypass reduced seed set; the Carvalho bypass caused necrosis. Most field trials are "limited by small size and low statistical power".
   - A teaching paradox: AP3 fully decarboxylates glycolate, losing more carbon per oxygenation, yet it helps because CO₂ is released inside the chloroplast. This bridges directly to C07–C08.

## Gaps

1. **Fate of fixed carbon (essential; it delivers the L01 "bread" promise).** C04 has one sentence. Suggested content for a static panel, about 60–90 s, in C04 or in place of a C12 subsection:
   - Triose-P leaves the chloroplast on the TPT in exchange for Pi, and cytosolic sucrose synthesis returns that Pi (Sharkey 2024; Flügge 1999, [10.1146/annurev.arplant.50.1.27](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.arplant.50.1.27)).
   - Transitory starch is made in the chloroplast from F6P. At night it is degraded to β-maltose and exported via MEX1 (Weise et al. 2004, [10.1007/s00425-003-1128-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00425-003-1128-y); Niittylä et al. 2004, Zeeman lab, [10.1126/science.1091811](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1091811)). The circadian clock sets degradation so starch lasts until dawn (Graf et al. 2010, [10.1073/pnas.0914299107](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0914299107); Smith & Zeeman 2020, [10.1146/annurev-arplant-050718-100241](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-arplant-050718-100241)).
   - Phloem loading: SWEET efflux followed by SUT/SUC uptake (Braun 2022, [10.1146/annurev-arplant-070721-083240](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-arplant-070721-083240)).
   - Wheat-specific trap to avoid: do **not** draw "leaf starch → grain starch". In wheat and barley, diurnal storage is mainly sucrose, and longer-term reserves are stem fructans (Schnyder 1993, [10.1111/j.1469-8137.1993.tb03731.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8137.1993.tb03731.x)). The ear also photosynthesises and supplies grain filling (Sanchez-Bragado et al. 2020, [10.1016/j.pbi.2020.01.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pbi.2020.01.001)).
   - Grain starch is rebuilt in the endosperm from imported sucrose. In grass endosperm, ADP-glucose is made mainly by a *cytosolic* AGPase, unlike other starch-storing organs (Beckles, Smith & ap Rees 2001, [10.1104/pp.125.2.818](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.125.2.818)).
2. **Light activation of the cycle (essential for later specialisation; currently reserve only).** One sentence and one icon row would cover it:
   - Fd → FTR → Trx reduces FBPase, SBPase, PRK, GAPDH/CP12 and activase-α (Yoshida & Hisabori 2023; Zhang & Portis 1999).
   - Rubisco must be carbamylated (activator CO₂ + Mg²⁺, Lorimer et al. 1976), and activase uses ATP to clear inhibitory sugar phosphates (Amaral et al. 2024).
   - In darkness an oxidising pathway switches the targets off.
3. **"Dark reactions" misnomer (present but weak).** Replace "does not mean the cycle waits for night" with: the cycle is *light-activated* and largely switched off in darkness. Use Buchanan's term "carbon reactions" (Buchanan 2016, [10.1007/s11120-015-0212-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11120-015-0212-z)).
4. **Smaller gaps.**
   - Temperature raises oxygenation. Both S_c/o and the CO₂/O₂ solubility ratio fall as temperature rises. One sentence is needed because the C₄ and bypass-in-heat stories depend on it (Walker et al. 2016, [10.1146/annurev-arplant-043015-111709](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-arplant-043015-111709)). Their model gives photorespiration costs of 36 % for US soybean and 20 % for wheat yield; this is model-based.
   - Rubisco abundance and nitrogen cost: about 0.7 Gt worldwide and about 3 % of leaf dry mass (Bar-On & Milo 2019, [10.1073/pnas.1816654116](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1816654116)).
   - Direct evidence that photorespiration is essential: *pglp* mutants cannot grow in air (Somerville & Ogren 1979) and 2-PG inhibits TPI and SBPase (Flügel et al. 2017).
   - Terminology: Sharkey 2024 uses GAP for glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate because "G3P" is also used for glycerol 3-phosphate. Use "triose phosphate" or GAP and avoid "G3P".

**Too technical for this audience** (keep in instructor context): the 3 Vc + 3.5 Vo and 2 Vc + 2 Vo terms and "NADPH equivalents"; the Degen 2026 pmf/ATP-pool argument; the FTR-mutant induction detail (Yoshida 2024); Prywes' deep mutational scan; the Bouvier-versus-Tcherkez method debate. The drafts already mostly do this.

## Story candidates (then → now)

- **S1 (recommended): proving a cycle with two switches.**
  - Then: a short ¹⁴C pulse first labels 3-PGA (Calvin & Benson 1948, [10.1126/science.107.2784.476](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.107.2784.476)). Steady-state transients settle the cycle: when the light goes off, PGA rises and RuBP falls; when CO₂ is lowered, RuBP rises and PGA falls (Calvin & Massini 1952, [10.1007/BF02139287](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02139287); Wilson & Calvin 1955, [10.1021/ja01627a050](https://doi.org/10.1021/ja01627a050); outline in Bassham et al. 1954). The directions of change are confirmed only by the Bassham 2003 retrospective abstract plus standard accounts; I did not read the primary texts.
  - Caution: Sharkey 2019 says the textbook picture of the "lollipop" vessel being used for the shortest labelling times is a simplification ([10.1007/s11120-018-0600-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11120-018-0600-2)). Do not animate the lollipop as the apparatus for 2–5 s pulses without checking the full text.
  - Now: ¹³CO₂ isotopically non-stationary flux analysis (INST-MFA) quantifies fluxes in the leaf. Ma et al. 2014 ([10.1073/pnas.1319485111](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319485111)) applied it to Arabidopsis leaves; Fu et al. 2023 and Gashu et al. 2025 used it to show serine and C1 exits from photorespiration.
  - Animation: two toggles (light off, CO₂ down) with PGA and RuBP pool bars, about 25 s.
- **S2 (recommended): from oxygenase to field bypasses.**
  - Then: Bowes, Ogren & Hageman 1971. Science rejected the work, Nature published it, and debates followed (Bowes' testimonial, https://aspb.org/membership/aspb-pioneer-members/pioneer-testimonials/william-ogren-testimonials/). Somerville & Ogren's 1979 air-lethal *pglp* mutant then showed photorespiration is essential.
  - Now: South et al. 2019 (+20–24 % tobacco biomass in the field), Cavanagh et al. 2022 (larger benefit under warming), Meacham-Hensold et al. 2024 (potato heatwaves), and the 2026 review's caution about small, low-power trials. Use the full-decarboxylation paradox as the bridge to C₂.
- **S3: an air-sick mutant reveals Rubisco activase.**
  - Then: Somerville, Portis & Ogren 1982 found a high-CO₂-requiring mutant that cannot activate Rubisco ([10.1104/pp.70.2.381](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.70.2.381)); the missing protein was identified by Salvucci, Portis & Ogren 1985 ([10.1007/BF00037012](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00037012)).
  - Now: Rubisco deactivation co-limits photosynthesis in heat worldwide (Scafaro et al. 2023), and activase isoforms are an engineering target (Amaral et al. 2024).
- **S4: from "dark" to "carbon" reactions.**
  - Then: Wolosiuk & Buchanan 1977 ([10.1038/266565a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/266565a0)).
  - Now: the oxidising "off" switch (Yoshida et al. 2018) and Buchanan's 2016 renaming.
- **S5: the "bad enzyme" revisited.**
  - Then: trade-off and "inefficient catalyst" framing (Andersson 2008).
  - Now: Flamholz et al. 2019, Bouvier et al. 2024, Prywes et al. 2025, and ML-directed evolution of tobacco Rubisco (Science Advances 2026).
- **S6: a leaf's night-time pantry, then a wheat grain.**
  - Then: night-time starch breakdown exported as maltose (Weise et al. 2004; Niittylä et al. 2004).
  - Now: the clock-timed starch budget (Graf et al. 2010) and cytosolic ADP-glucose synthesis in cereal endosperm (Beckles et al. 2001). This closes the "bread" loop.
