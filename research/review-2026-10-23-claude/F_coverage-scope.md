# F — Coverage and scope review of the photosynthesis sequence (L01–L15, C01–C12)

Independent review, 23 October 2026. Read-only on the project. The checklist below was built from textbook tables of contents and ETH course pages before mapping the 27 slots, so it does not inherit the drafting agent's selection. Every reference was checked against Europe PMC or Crossref metadata (abstract level unless stated). Items I could not check are marked **UNVERIFIED**.

## 0. Bottom line

- The mechanistic core is well covered, sometimes more than a BSc class with no background needs. Electron-route accounting (L09–L11) and the C₂ narrative (C07–C08, 6 min) are the heaviest parts.
- **Missing essentials** (all appear in the anchor TOCs):
  - Where photosynthesis happens: the leaf-to-chloroplast zoom.
  - Evolutionary origin: cyanobacteria, the Great Oxidation Event (GOE) and endosymbiosis.
  - Fate of the product: triose-phosphate export, sucrose versus starch, and the phloem.
  - Global scale.
  - Water/δ¹³C signatures of the C₃, C₄ and CAM pathways.
  - Photosynthetic efficiency and yield.

  The last two connect directly to the stated objective of ETH 751-3700-00L: "analyze agricultural yield potential".
- All approved hooks fit at 45 min per lecture without touching the six approved opening slides (L01–L06). Lecture 1 trims L09–L12 by 2.5 min. Lecture 2 folds CAM into C₄, merges C07 into C08, and trims C11, C12 and C04. Tables are in §3.4.
- **Honest status of the crop hooks:**
  - VPZ (faster NPQ relaxation): the mechanism works reliably, but yield gains are inconsistent.
  - Photorespiratory bypasses: consistently positive in published trials, but mostly biomass, tobacco or per-plant yield, and the mechanism is still unresolved.
  - Rubisco: overexpression works in rice paddies. No catalytically improved Rubisco has yet reached a crop field.
  - C₄ rice: no functional C₄ rice after about 15 years.

## 3. New approved scope: concrete proposals

### 3.1 (a) Crop improvement: honest hooks

**VPZ: faster NPQ relaxation.** Slot L13; **+1 min**, ~45–60 s spoken.
- **Opener:** the theoretical maximum solar-to-biomass conversion is 4.6% for C₃ and 6% for C₄ ([Zhu et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.copbio.2008.02.004)). Slow NPQ relaxation after sun-to-shade transitions was calculated to cost up to 20% of yield.
- **Tobacco 2016:** about +15% dry matter in fluctuating light, in the field ([Kromdijk et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aai8878)).
- **Soybean 2022** ([De Souza et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adc9831); checked in the paper text):
  - 2020 season: 5 of 8 events gave higher seed yield, mean +24.5%, best +33%.
  - 2021 season: no significant yield gain. The authors cite storm lodging and fewer sun-to-shade transitions.
  - Disputed as "flawed" by [Sinclair et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ade8506).
- **Failures and nulls:**
  - Arabidopsis: growth impaired ([Garcia-Molina & Leister 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-019-0572-z)).
  - Potato: no gain; tuber yield fell under fluctuating light ([Lehretz et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/jipb.13320)).
  - Soybean field 2026 (line VPZ-34A): better light-use efficiency, but no gain in assimilation or seed yield at ambient or elevated CO₂ ([Singh et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.70608)).
- **Message:** the mechanism transfers reliably; the yield benefit depends on species, season and sink. This is why multi-year field trials matter.

**Photorespiratory bypasses.** Slot C06; **+0.5 min**.
- **Field results:**
  - [Kebeish et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt1299): a chloroplast glycolate bypass raised chloroplast CO₂ near Rubisco.
  - [South et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat9077): tobacco field trials; quantum yield +20%, biomass >40%. The pathways were combined with blocking glycolate export.
  - [Cavanagh et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/pbi.13750): +26% biomass under +5 °C versus +11% at ambient.
  - Rice GCBG bypass: +19% yield ([Chen et al. 2024/25](https://doi.org/10.1093/plcell/koaf015)).
  - Earlier rice GOC/GCGT bypasses had reduced seed-setting. Potato GOC gave +21–69% yield per plant across Chinese field sites ([Lin et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/pbi.70076)).
- **Open questions:** energetics, flux behaviour and the basis of thermotolerance are unresolved; multi-environment, breeder-style trials are still needed ([Meacham-Hensold et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.70724)).
- **My caveat (not from a source):** evidence is mostly tobacco biomass or per-plant yield, with few independent replications in major grain crops.
- **Teaching bridge:** the bypass releases or concentrates CO₂ *inside the chloroplast*, which is engineered C₂ logic. That leads straight into C07/C08.

**Rubisco engineering plus abundance.** Slot C05; **+0.5 min**.
- **Abundance:** ≈0.7 Gt globally, >90% of it in land-plant leaves, ≈3% of leaf mass. Its time-averaged rate on land is ≈0.03 s⁻¹, about 100-fold below the laboratory rate ([Bar-On & Milo 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1816654116)).
- **Engineering record:**
  - Cyanobacterial Rubisco installed in tobacco ([Lin et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13776)).
  - More Rubisco raised maize Rubisco content ([Salesse-Smith et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-018-0252-4)) and rice yield with sufficient N in a paddy field ([Yoon et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43016-020-0033-x)).
  - Better algal Rubiscos have so far failed to fold in plants; crop directed evolution is underway ([Gionfriddo et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semcdb.2023.04.003); [Prywes et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-biochem-040320-101244)).
- **Message:** more enzyme works; a better enzyme has not yet been field-tested.

**C₄ rice.** Slot C09; within its +1 min.
- The Gates-funded project was "in its 13th year" in 2023 ([Furbank et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11120-023-01014-0)).
- Five maize C₄ enzymes were installed in rice, but PEPC flux was ≈2% of maize's ([Ermakova et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/pbi.13487)).
- Maize PEPC in rice works at <2.1% of its in-vitro activity in planta, held back by regulatory mismatch (malate inhibition, phosphorylation in the dark) ([Om et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiag387)).
- A bundle-sheath regulatory module was found in rice ([Hua et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-62087-0)). GOLDEN2-LIKE expression gives proto-Kranz-like bundle-sheath organelles ([Wang et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2017.09.040)).
- **Status:** no functional C₄ rice yet, and Kranz anatomy has not been engineered.

**RIPE.** One line at C06; 0 min.
- An international consortium (Illinois-led; Gates, FFAR and UK funding).
- Since December 2022: a $34 M, 4-year grant focused on cowpea and soybean ([RIPE, 13 Dec 2022](https://ripe.illinois.edu/node/694)).
- Status after 2026: **UNVERIFIED**.

### 3.2 (b) Sugar export: closing the "bread that feeds us" promise

**New C04b, "Leaving the chloroplast: sucrose by day, starch for the night" (2 min).** Place it after C04, because TPU in C12 needs it.
- **Export:** triose phosphate leaves via the triose-phosphate/phosphate translocator in strict exchange for Pi ([Flügge 1999](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.arplant.50.1.27)).
  - Sucrose is made in the cytosol. If its synthesis lags, Pi returns too slowly and ATP synthesis slows. That is the basis of TPU in C12.
  - The chloroplast, a former endosymbiont, supplies sucrose to its host ([Sharkey 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiad601)). This links back to the L07 endosymbiosis hook.
- **Starch:** part of the carbon is stored as transitory starch and exported at night as maltose ([Niittylä et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1091811)).
  - The night-time degradation rate is set so reserves last almost exactly until dawn: "arithmetic division" ([Graf et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0914299107); [Scialdone et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.00669); [Smith & Zeeman 2020](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-arplant-050718-100241)).
  - ETH connection: Zeeman's group works on starch; Santelia co-teaches ETH-PE.
- **Phloem:** sucrose efflux by SWEETs, then loading ([Chen et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1213351); [Braun 2022](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-arplant-070721-083240)). Bulk flow follows Münch's 1930 pressure-flow hypothesis, supported by [Knoblauch et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.15341).

**New C13, "From leaf to loaf" (1 min closing).** It reconnects to the L01 opening.
- In high-yielding wheat, the ear contributed more to grain filling than the shoot; the flag-leaf blade gave only 3–18% ([Sanchez-Bragado et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/eru298); [Simkin et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/tpj.14633)). Stem reserves also contribute ([Ehdaie et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fcr.2007.10.012); magnitudes not checked).
- In the endosperm, sucrose → ADP-glucose → amyloplast starch. Starch synthesis is the major determinant of cereal yield ([Emes et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/erg089)).
- Wheat supplies about 20% of global dietary calories and protein (Shiferaw et al. 2013, *Food Security* 5:291, [doi:10.1007/s12571-013-0263-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12571-013-0263-y); abstract-level via search, not opened).
